# Lesson of the day, 2026-10-05

**You own 0.074 of a CrowdStrike share: what does that mean?**

A **share** is one equal slice of a company. Owning shares makes you a part-owner: when the
company grows more valuable, the price of each slice tends to rise, and when it pays out cash
(a dividend), each slice gets the same amount.

A **fractional share** is a piece of one slice. Your broker records that you own, say, 0.074 of
one CrowdStrike share. This is what let you spread $199.47 across 16 companies: one whole ASML
share costs $1,859.86, more than nine times your whole portfolio.

A fraction behaves exactly like a whole share, just scaled down:

- **Same percentage return.** On Monday a whole CrowdStrike share rose 0.97%, from $270.04 to
  $272.67, a gain of $2.63. A 0.074 slice held through that day would have moved
  0.074 × $2.63 ≈ $0.19. That is also 0.97%.
- **Same dividend per share, scaled.** Agilent pays 25.5 cents per share. Your 0.03 shares would
  get 0.03 × 25.5 ≈ 0.77 cents.

So owning less than one share does not change how good or bad an investment is. It only
changes how many dollars ride on it. That is why this report always shows both: the percentage
(how the company did) and the dollars (what it meant for you).

*Check yourself:* if CrowdStrike rose 10% from Monday's close of $272.67, what would your
0.074 shares be worth, and how many dollars would you have gained? (Answer at the end of the
report.)
