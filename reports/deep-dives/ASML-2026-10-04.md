## Plain-English summary (for Santi)

**The one-liner:** ASML is the only company in the world that makes the machines needed to print the most advanced computer chips. Business is booming because of AI. But the share price already assumes many more years of fast growth, so the risk is less "is this a great company?" (it is) and more "have I paid too much for it?".

**What it sells.** Chips are made by shining light through a stencil onto a silicon disc, a bit like a slide projector printing a pattern. The smaller the pattern, the more powerful the chip. ASML builds those "projectors" (lithography machines \[machines that print chip patterns with light\]). Its top model uses extreme ultraviolet light (EUV \[a very short wavelength of light that can print tiny details\]). Nobody else sells EUV machines [20-F FY2025, Business section]. Each one is the size of a bus.

**Who buys and how it makes money.** A handful of chipmakers (TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Chinese fabs). Four customers each bought more than 10% of everything ASML sold in 2025, together 61.2% [20-F FY2025, geographic/segment note]. About 75% of sales are new machines and 25% is servicing and upgrading machines already installed [20-F FY2025, Item 5: €24,474.3m systems vs €8,193.0m service].

**What the numbers say.** 2025 sales were €32.7bn, up 15.6%, and it kept about 29 cents of profit from each euro of sales [20-F FY2025]. In July it raised its 2026 forecast to €43–45bn [6-K, 2026-07-15], from €36–40bn three months earlier [6-K, 2026-04-15]: AI chip demand is pulling orders forward.

**Case for:** a monopoly in the most important step of chipmaking, high profits, net cash, and a growing pile of service revenue. **Case against:** the price. At about 60 times the last 12 months of profit, versus 34–37 times at the end of each of the last four years, today's price assumes cash flow grows about 19% a year for ten years (our estimate). Also: 29% of 2025 sales went to China, which governments can restrict at any time.

**Biggest open risk right now:** a proposed US law (the MATCH Act) could stop ASML selling older machines to China and servicing those already there. It is not law, and its status is unconfirmed (tier-3 reports only).

**What would prove the thesis wrong:** the 2026 forecast cut, gross margin falling back below 50%, or new export rules that block sales to China.

**New words in this deep dive:** *P/E* (share price ÷ yearly profit per share: how many years of today's profit you pay for); *reverse DCF* (working backwards from the price to the growth it assumes); *gross margin* (what is left of each euro of sales after the cost of making the product); *free cash flow* (cash left after running and investing in the business); *contract liabilities* (customer deposits for machines not yet delivered); *export controls* (government rules on what may be sold to which countries).

*Analysis, not advice. The decision is yours.*

## VERDICT AND KEY RISKS

**Verdict:** An exceptional, monopoly-protected business in a strong upswing, priced for many more years of fast growth. Business quality high; valuation stretched against its own history; margin of safety thin. Confidence: high on the business facts, low on any valuation call.

**Composite score:** 7.1 / 10 (score.py, semiconductors preset, unedited default benchmarks) · **Sector playbook applied:** semiconductors · **Situation flags:** cyclical upswing (equipment orders follow chipmaker spending)

**The three things that matter most:**
1. **Monopoly in EUV.** Sole supplier; 48 EUV systems recognised in 2025 vs 44 in 2024 [20-F FY2025, KPIs]. Gross margin 52.8% in 2025, 54.0% in Q2 2026 [20-F; 6-K 2026-07-15].
2. **Demand is accelerating.** 2026 sales guidance €43–45bn [6-K 2026-07-15] vs €32,667.3m in 2025 [20-F], about +32% to +38% [computed]. Capacity for 2027 planned about 30% higher for low-NA EUV and DUV immersion [6-K 2026-07-15].
3. **The price assumes a lot.** Trailing P/E 60.0x [valuation.py: €1,658.8 / TTM diluted EPS ~€27.5], against 34.3x–37.3x at the last four year-ends [computed from 20-F share price table and diluted EPS]. Reverse DCF: ~18.9% a year FCF growth for 10 years at a 9% discount rate [valuation.py].

**Key risks — what could break this:**
1. **Valuation compression.** If the P/E went back to its 2022–2025 year-end range (34–37x) with profits unchanged, the price would fall roughly 40% [computed: 35/60 − 1]. No fundamental change is needed for this.
2. **China / export controls.** China was €9,519.7m, 29.1% of 2025 sales [20-F FY2025, geographic note], and about 20% of 2026 sales per management [Q2 2026 call, search summary]. Dutch and US licence rules decide what can ship. A pending US bill, the MATCH Act (introduced 2026-04-02), would reportedly push allies to stop sales of older DUV immersion machines to major Chinese chipmakers *and* servicing of machines already installed there; it was reported in August as heading into the Senate defence bill, and the Dutch prime minister said on 2026-09-25 he was "very confident" of a deal [NL Times, 2026-08-20; De Belegger; DIGITIMES 2026-09-25 — all tier 3, not confirmed by a primary source]. Status in October 2026: not law; unconfirmed.
3. **Customer concentration and cycle.** Largest customer 23.9% of 2025 sales, top two 38.0% [20-F FY2025, risk factors]. If one big chipmaker pauses spending, orders fall fast: equipment is the most cyclical part of the chip chain [semiconductors playbook].

**What would change the verdict:** see Section 10.

## 1. The Business — What It Sells and How It Makes Money

- **What customers buy and why ASML.** Lithography systems: EUV (NXE low-NA and EXE high-NA models) for the finest chip layers, and DUV (deep ultraviolet) immersion and dry systems for the rest. Plus metrology and inspection tools, and "installed base management": service contracts and upgrades. Chipmakers choose ASML because, at the leading edge, there is no alternative EUV supplier [20-F FY2025, Business].
- **Revenue build.** Units × price, plus service. 2025: 327 lithography systems sold (418 in 2024), of which 48 EUV [20-F FY2025, KPIs]; 4 EXE (high-NA) and 44 NXE systems recognised [20-F FY2025, Item 5]. Fewer units but higher sales means the mix shifted to expensive EUV machines.
- **Mix.** Net system sales €24,474.3m (74.9%), +12.4%; service and field options €8,193.0m (25.1%), +26.2% [20-F FY2025, Item 5 results table]. Service grows as the installed base grows, and it is steadier than new-machine sales.
- **Where the money goes.** Cost of sales 47.2% of sales, R&D 14.4%, SG&A 3.9% → operating margin 34.6% [20-F FY2025, Item 5]. R&D of €4,698.8m is mostly fixed, so higher sales lift margins (operating leverage).
- **Cash path.** Customers pay large deposits years ahead: contract liabilities (money received for machines not yet delivered) were €19,372.6m at end-2025 [20-F XBRL, ContractWithCustomerLiability]. This makes cash flow lumpy: €11,410.3m operating cash in Q4 2025, then −€2,185.6m in Q1 2026 [6-K 2026-07-15, quarterly summary].

## 2. Sector Classification and Playbook Applied

**Sector / sub-sector:** Semiconductor equipment (wafer-fab equipment, lithography). **Playbook:** `references/sectors/semiconductors.md`.

**Why:** ASML sells tools to fabs; its earnings follow chipmakers' capital spending with a lag, which is the equipment pattern in the playbook.

| Standard metric | Status here | Why |

## 3. Situation Classification

**Situation:** Cyclical company in an upswing. Equipment orders are "the derivative of the derivative" of chip demand [semiconductors playbook]. **Implication:** today's earnings are not mid-cycle; a P/E on peak-ish earnings understates risk. Scenarios below include a downturn case.

## 7. Valuation

**Method:** reverse DCF cross-checked against ASML's own P/E history. **Why:** stable, highly cash-generative, asset-light; FCF is meaningful. **Inputs:** base FCF €11,027.3m (FY2025, less lumpy than TTM); discount rate 9% (analyst assumption: no euro risk-free rate was sourced this session, so treat it as a round, conservative-ish figure); terminal growth 3%; 10-year stage 1.

### Reverse-DCF: what the current price implies
At €1,658.8 (about $1,867), the market is pricing in roughly **18.9% a year free-cash-flow growth for ten years**, then 3% forever [valuation.py]. **Reality check:** FCF grew about 15.4% a year 2022–2025 (€7,167.5m → €11,027.3m, computed) and sales about 18.5% a year 2020–2025. So the price asks for slightly *more* than ASML's very good recent record, for twice as long, with no downturn. Possible given AI demand; not a margin of safety.

## 9. The Bear Case

The bull case needs three things to stay true at once: AI chip demand keeps rising for years, China keeps buying at about a fifth to a third of sales, and investors keep paying about 60 times earnings. History says at least one usually breaks. Chip equipment is the most cyclical link in the chain: when chipmakers have built enough capacity they cut orders sharply, and ASML's 2024 (sales +2.6%, 20-F) shows how quickly growth can stall even for a monopoly. ASML is now adding about 30% EUV capacity for 2027 [6-K 2026-07-15], which is exactly what equipment makers do near cycle tops. China sales were inflated in 2024–2025 by stock-up buying ahead of restrictions; ASML itself expected China to fall back [Caixin Global, 2026-01-29, search summary only]. If growth merely slows to the 2022–2025 pace and the P/E returns to its own 35x average, the shares fall about 40% with no "bad news" at all. And with four customers at 61% of sales, ASML's growth is really TSMC's and Samsung's capex plan, which ASML does not control.

**Strongest counter to the bear case:** the company raised 2026 guidance twice in four months and said on its Q2 2026 call that it is close to having all the 2027 orders it needs [Q2 2026 earnings call, 2026-07-15; transcript not read directly, so treat as medium confidence], so near-term demand is visible; and service revenue (25% of sales, +26%) cushions downturns.
