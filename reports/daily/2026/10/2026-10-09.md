## Lesson of the day

**Why did half your portfolio fall together on Thursday?**

On Thursday, chip and optical stocks fell in the morning (oil, high rates, profit-taking), and then a newspaper report said OpenAI's revenue pace was lower than people thought. Your 11 holdings tied to AI data centers held $121.98 on Wednesday, 53.5% of your money. On Thursday they lost **$5.86, or 4.8%**. Your other 6 holdings (Moderna, Agilent, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Snowflake and QQQM) lost **$0.60, or 0.57%**. The same worries hit the whole group at once.

The reason is **correlation**: how often two stocks move the same way on the same day. It runs from −1 to +1. At +1 they move in lockstep, at 0 they ignore each other. Lumentum and VIAVI have a 60-day correlation of 0.84. CrowdStrike and Palo Alto have 0.92. Agilent and your chip stocks have at most 0.22. Stocks are highly correlated when the same thing drives them: the same customers (the big AI spenders), the same budgets, the same worries.

**Worked example.** You own four optical stocks (Lumentum, AAOI, AXT and VIAVI), worth $45.81 on Wednesday. On Thursday they lost $3.54 together (−7.7%), and on Friday three of them bounced together. Four tickers, but on a day like Thursday they behaved like one bigger bet. Diversification depends on owning things that do *not* share the same driver, not on the number of tickers.

## New words today

- **Annualized revenue (run rate)**: today's sales pace stretched to a full year. OpenAI's "about $50 billion" is a pace, not a year of reported sales.
- **Warrant**: a right to buy a company's shares at a fixed price, often very low, sometimes unlocked in stages. If used, new shares are created, so existing shares own a smaller slice.
- **Personalised cancer vaccine**: a vaccine made for one patient from a reading of their own tumour's genes, teaching the immune system to attack that tumour.
- **Social engineering**: tricking a person (for example by pretending to be a colleague) into handing over a password, instead of breaking the software.
- **"Sold out" capacity**: customers have already reserved everything a factory can make for a period, so sales are more predictable and prices are easier to hold.
- **Pricing power**: a seller's ability to keep or raise prices without losing customers, usually because demand exceeds supply.
- **Book-to-burn**: new work signed divided by work completed in a period. Above 1 means the pile of future work is growing.
- **Monthly revenue**: sales published each month (TSMC does this), giving investors an early look before quarterly results.
- **Preliminary results**: early, rounded figures a company (Samsung here) publishes before its full quarterly report.
- **Employee stock purchase plan**: a company program that buys shares for staff on preset terms. Purchases under it are routine, not a signal.
- **GRAT and gift (code G)**: a GRAT is a type of family trust used to pass shares to relatives. Moving shares into it is reported as a gift (code G), not a sale; no money changes hands.
- **Charitable foundation (as a seller)**: a charity that receives donated shares and may sell them. If an executive controls it, its sales are reported like an insider's.
- **Interposer / CoWoS**: an interposer is a thin silicon base on which an AI processor and its memory chips sit side by side, wired together; CoWoS is TSMC's name for this way of packaging chips.
- **Relative weakness**: a stock doing worse than its own industry even on good days.
- **Momentum**: buying a stock because it has been rising. It can push prices beyond what new facts justify, in both directions.
- **50-day average**: the average closing price over the last 50 trading days; breaking below it sometimes triggers selling by traders who follow trends.
- **Compressed natural gas (trucked gas)**: natural gas squeezed into tanks and delivered by truck when no pipeline exists. It works, but costs more.
- **Bookings**: the value of new orders signed in a period, a window into future sales. ASML stopped publishing them quarterly in 2026.
- **Laser chip**: the tiny laser inside an optical module that turns electrical signals into light; built on wafers like AXT's.
- **Fab**: short for fabrication plant, a chip factory. A new one takes years to build.
- **OCP (Open Compute Project) Global Summit**: a yearly data-center hardware conference where big buyers and suppliers show new designs.
- **ESMO**: the European Society for Medical Oncology, whose yearly congress is a main stage for new cancer-trial results.
- **NIH**: the National Institutes of Health, the US government's medical research agency.
- **Public-private partnership**: a project run jointly by a government agency and companies, sharing cost and work.
- **Trading volume**: the number of shares that changed hands in a day. "Twice its usual volume" means unusually many people were buying and selling.
- **Analyst labels (Buy, Outperform, Hold, Sell)**: an analyst's opinion of how a stock will do against the market or similar stocks. They are opinions; they matter mainly when they bring new information.
- **Gigawatt**: a billion watts, roughly one large power station. AI deals are often measured by the power the equipment will use.
- **High-bandwidth memory (HBM)**: stacks of memory chips placed right next to an AI processor so data reaches it fast.
- **DRAM**: the main working memory in computers and servers; HBM is a stacked form of it.
- **mRNA**: a genetic message that tells cells which protein to make. Moderna's vaccines are built on it.
- **Co-packaged optics**: light-based links built into the same package as a chip, instead of a separate plug-in module.
- **Dividend**: cash a company pays its shareholders, usually every quarter.
- **Fed (Federal Reserve)**: the US central bank. It sets short-term interest rates, which affect what investors pay for stocks.
