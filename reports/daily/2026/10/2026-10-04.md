## Lesson of the day

**Why won't your weekend orders fill at Friday's price?**

Your sheet lists 16 orders dated Monday 5 October, each with an "entry price" that is really Friday's closing price. US stock markets are closed at weekends, so nothing trades until Monday at 9:30 a.m. New York time (15:30 in Madrid).

A **market order** says "fill my order right away, at whatever the price is." When the market opens, your order fills at the first available price. That can be higher or lower than Friday's close, because weekend news changes what buyers and sellers will pay. A **limit order** says "buy only at this price or better." It protects you from a surprise price, but it may never fill if the stock runs away from your limit.

*Worked example:* your sheet assumes Lumentum (LITE) at $1,085.42, so 0.018 shares cost $19.54. If LITE opens 3% higher on Monday, at about $1,117.98, the same 0.018 shares cost about $20.12: 58 cents more before the market has done anything "to" you. That is why the report shows zero profit or loss today: you do not own these shares yet. Once you know your real Fidelity fill prices, type them into column Q of your sheet so the report can measure from the price you actually paid.

## New words today

- **Guidance**: a company's own forecast of its future sales or profit; markets react to it more than to last quarter's results.
- **Consensus**: the average forecast of the analysts who follow a company; "beating" means doing better than it.
- **EPS (earnings per share)**: a company's profit divided by its number of shares, i.e. your slice of the profit for each share.
- **Gross margin**: what is left of each dollar of sales after paying to make the product; 54% means 54 cents of every dollar.
- **Operating margin**: what is left after also paying for research, sales and offices.
- **P/E (price-to-earnings)**: share price divided by yearly EPS, i.e. how many years of today's profit you pay for. 60 means 60 years' worth.
- **GAAP / non-GAAP**: GAAP is the official accounting rulebook; "non-GAAP" or "adjusted" numbers leave some costs out and usually look better.
- **Market cap**: share price times the number of shares, which is what the market says the whole company is worth.
- **Backlog**: signed orders not yet delivered, i.e. work already lined up.
- **Capex**: money spent on long-lasting things like factories and machines.
- **ETF**: a fund that holds a basket of stocks and trades like one share; SMH holds chip companies and is used here as "the chip industry".
- **Beta**: how much a stock tends to move when the market moves; 2 means about twice as much.
- **Volatility**: how bumpy a stock's ride is; higher means bigger daily swings, both ways.
- **Correlation**: how closely two stocks move together, from −1 (opposite) to 1 (in lockstep).
- **Drawdown**: a fall from a previous high, e.g. "down 54% at its worst".
- **Moving average**: the average price over the last 50 or 200 days; being above it is read as an uptrend.
- **RSI**: a 0–100 gauge of how fast a stock has risen or fallen lately; above 70 means a very fast rise.
- **Value at risk (VaR)**: a loss that is exceeded only on the worst 1 day in 20, based on past behaviour.
- **8-K / 10-Q / 10-K**: US company filings: an 8-K reports an important event, a 10-Q is the quarterly report, a 10-K the annual report.
- **Form 4 / Form 144**: filings showing that an insider (director or officer) traded shares, or plans to sell.
- **10b5-1**: a pre-planned trading schedule an insider sets months ahead, so a sale under it says little about today's view.
- **Dilution**: when a company creates new shares, so each existing share owns a smaller slice.
- **Buyback**: when a company buys its own shares, so each remaining share owns a bigger slice.
- **Export controls**: government rules about what may be sold to which countries.
- **ARR (annual recurring revenue)**: the yearly value of the subscriptions customers have signed.
- **Net new ARR**: how much ARR grew during one quarter, after subtracting customers who left.
- **Net revenue retention**: what last year's customers spend this year, compared with last year; 126% means they spend 26% more.
- **Free cash flow**: cash left over after paying the bills and investing in equipment, i.e. money the company could hand back or save.
- **GPU**: a graphics processor, a chip that does many small calculations at once, which suits AI.
- **Hyperscaler**: the giant cloud companies (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle) that build the biggest data centers.
- **Active electrical cable**: a copper cable with a small chip at each end that strengthens the signal so it travels further.
- **Optical transceiver**: a plug-in module that turns electrical signals into light for fibre-optic cables, and back.
- **Attribution (market vs company)**: splitting a day's move into the part from the whole market, the part from the stock's industry, and the part from the company itself.
- **Z-score**: how unusual a move is compared with the stock's normal swings; 1 means one typical swing, and only values well above 2 are rare.
- **52-week high**: the highest price a stock reached in the past year.
- **Thesis / breaker**: your thesis is your written reason for owning a stock; a breaker is an event you listed that would make you re-check it.
- **Stop-loss / target**: prices you set in advance to be alerted if a stock falls (stop-loss) or rises (target) that far.
- **Price target**: the price an analyst expects a stock to reach in about a year; an opinion, not a fact.
- **Analyst rating / downgrade**: an analyst's label (Buy, Outperform, Neutral, Equal-Weight, Sell); a downgrade lowers it.
- **Convertible bonds (convertible notes)**: loans to a company that the lender can later swap for shares, which can dilute owners.
- **Capped call**: a side deal with banks that cancels out the new shares from convertible bonds, up to a ceiling price.
- **Shelf filing (S-3)**: a registration that lets a company sell new shares quickly whenever it chooses.
- **Securities class action**: a group lawsuit by shareholders who say the company misled them.
- **Force majeure**: a contract clause that excuses delays caused by events outside a party's control.
- **Index fund / rebalance**: a fund that automatically owns every stock in a list (an index); it rebalances by buying and selling when the list changes.
- **Nasdaq-100, S&P 500, MidCap 400, SmallCap 600**: well-known lists (indexes) of companies that many funds copy.
- **Phase 3**: the final, largest round of patient testing before a medicine can be approved.
- **Foundry**: a factory that manufactures chips designed by other companies.
- **Lithography / EUV**: printing circuit patterns onto silicon with light; EUV (extreme ultraviolet) is the most advanced kind.
- **Call option**: a contract that pays off only if a stock rises above a set price.
- **Momentum**: buyers chasing a price because it has been rising.
- **After hours**: trading after the regular 4 p.m. New York close.
- **Operating cash flow**: cash brought in by the day-to-day business.
- **Receivables**: money customers owe for goods already delivered.
- **Net cash**: more cash than debt.
- **Dividend / record date**: cash a company pays its owners; you must own shares on the record date to get it.
- **Fiscal year (FY)**: a company's own accounting year, which need not match the calendar.
- **Tier 1 / 2 / 3 sources**: tier 1 is the company or regulator itself, tier 2 established newsrooms, tier 3 secondary sites to confirm before relying on.
