# Plain-English glossary

One consistent definition per term. Use the wording, adapt the example to his holdings.

**Share / stock**: a small piece of ownership in a company. **Fractional share**: a part of one
share (0.074 shares = 7.4% of one share). **Ticker**: the short code a stock trades under (CRWD).

**Market capitalization (market cap)**: share price x number of shares; what the stock market
says the whole company is worth. **Enterprise value (EV)**: market cap plus debt minus cash; what
it would cost to buy the whole business, debts included.

**Revenue (sales)**: all the money customers paid for products and services in a period.
**Gross margin**: the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making what was sold.
**Operating margin**: the share of revenue left after all running costs (including research and
salaries), before interest and taxes. **Net income (profit)**: what is left after everything,
including taxes. **Earnings per share (EPS)**: net income divided by the number of shares.
**Free cash flow**: cash from running the business minus money spent on long-lived assets; the
cash a company could actually hand back or reinvest.

**GAAP**: the official US accounting rules (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles).
**Non-GAAP (adjusted)**: the company's own version that leaves out some costs, often employee
stock pay; useful for trends, but the company chooses what to leave out.
**Stock-based compensation**: paying employees with shares; a real cost that slowly dilutes owners.
**EBITDA**: earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization; a rough measure of
operating cash profit.

**Consensus (estimate)**: the average forecast of the analysts who follow the company.
**Beat / miss**: results above / below the consensus. **Guidance**: the company's own forecast
for the next quarter or year. **Priced in**: already reflected in today's price because investors
expected it.

**Price-to-earnings ratio (P/E)**: share price divided by yearly EPS; how many dollars investors
pay for $1 of yearly profit. **Forward P/E**: the same, using next year's expected EPS.
**Multiple**: shorthand for ratios like P/E. **Multiple compression / expansion**: investors
paying less / more per dollar of profit than before.

**Annual recurring revenue (ARR)**: the yearly value of active subscriptions. **Net new ARR**:
how much ARR was added in the period. **Net revenue retention (NRR)**: what existing customers
spend now versus a year ago (above 100% means they spend more). **Remaining performance
obligations (RPO)**: contracted revenue not yet recognised.

**Backlog**: signed work not yet done or billed. **Book-to-bill**: new orders divided by revenue
billed; above 1 means orders are arriving faster than sales.

**Data center**: a building full of computers rented over the internet. **Hyperscaler**: the
biggest cloud operators (Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle). **Capital expenditures (capex)**:
money spent on long-lived assets such as buildings, servers and equipment.

**Graphics processing unit (GPU)**: a chip that does thousands of simple calculations at once,
now the main engine for training and running AI. **ASIC (application-specific integrated
circuit)**: a chip designed for one job, such as a cloud company's own AI chip.
**Optical transceiver**: a device that converts electrical signals into laser light for fibre
cables and back. **Active electrical cable (AEC)**: a copper cable with built-in chips that keep
high-speed signals clean over longer distances. **SerDes**: the circuit that packs many data lines
into one very fast stream and unpacks it (serializer/deserializer).

**8-K**: a filing a company must make within four business days of a major event. **10-Q /
10-K**: the quarterly / annual report with financial statements. **Form 4**: an insider's report
of buying or selling shares, due within two business days. **Form 144**: notice that an insider
plans to sell restricted shares. **10b5-1 plan**: a trading plan scheduled in advance so insiders
can sell without being accused of using secret information.

**Dilution**: new shares reduce each existing share's slice of the company. **Buyback**: the
company buys its own shares, so each remaining share owns more.

**ETF (exchange-traded fund)**: a basket of stocks you can buy like one stock. **S&P 500**: an
index of about 500 large US companies, used as "the market". **Nasdaq-100**: an index of 100
large, mostly tech, companies on the Nasdaq exchange.

**Volatility**: how much a price typically swings. **Beta**: how much a stock has tended to move
when the market moves 1%. **Drawdown**: the % fall from a previous peak. **Moving average**: the
average price over the last N days. **Relative strength Index (RSI)**: a 0-100 gauge of recent
gains versus losses. **Correlation**: how much two prices move together (-1 to +1).
**Value at risk (VaR)**: a loss that, historically, was exceeded only on the worst 5% of days.

**Market order**: buy or sell now at the current price. **Limit order**: only at your price or
better. **Stop-loss**: a price at which you have decided to sell to limit a loss.

**Export controls**: government rules restricting the sale of certain technology to certain
countries. **Tariff**: a tax on imported goods.
