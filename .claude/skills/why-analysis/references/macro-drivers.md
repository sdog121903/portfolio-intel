# Macro and policy transmission chains

How a big-picture event reaches the holdings. Use the chain, then say which holdings are most
exposed and why. Always cite the event itself.

| Event | Chain to the companies | Most exposed here |
|---|---|---|
| **Interest rates rise** (or are expected to) | Future profits are worth less today -> investors pay less for companies whose profits are far in the future -> fast-growing, highly valued stocks fall more | All five; most for the most highly valued |
| **Big cloud companies raise or cut capital spending (capex)** | Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta and others buy chips, optics, cables and data-center buildings -> their budgets are these companies' revenue | NVDA, CRDO, LITE, STRL directly |
| **Chip export controls tighten** | Rules limit selling advanced chips to some countries -> fewer possible customers -> lower expected sales | NVDA most; suppliers indirectly |
| **Tariffs on imported components** | Higher input costs or retaliation -> lower margins or demand | Hardware makers with Asian supply chains |
| **A major cyberattack in the news** | Companies see the cost of weak security -> security budgets grow or shift vendors | CRWD (and its competitors) |
| **A security product failure** | Customers reconsider vendors -> renewals and new deals at risk | CRWD |
| **Construction costs or labor shortages** | Margins on fixed-price projects shrink | STRL |
| **"AI bubble" worries / a peer's disappointing results** | Investors fear AI spending peaks -> the whole AI supply chain sells off together, often regardless of each company's own news | NVDA, CRDO, LITE, STRL together (check correlation) |
| **Recession fears** | Companies delay big purchases -> orders slow | All; infrastructure and hardware first |

When explaining a sector-wide sell-off, show it with the attribution numbers ("the industry part
explains most of the move") and the portfolio correlation ("your AI holdings tend to move together").
