# The Why Ladder

Keep asking "why?" until you reach a basic driver or run out of evidence. Each rung is one short
sentence, with a source or a confidence label. Usually 3-5 rungs.

**Basic drivers** (stop when you reach one): demand, price, cost, competition, supply,
regulation, interest rates, management decisions, accounting, expectations.

## Worked example (hypothetical, to show the method)

1. **What happened:** Credo's stock fell 12% after results. [press release / Reuters]
2. **Why?** Revenue beat forecasts, but next quarter's forecast (guidance) was below what
   analysts expected. [earnings release, call transcript]
3. **Why does guidance matter more than the beat?** The share price is a bet on future profits;
   the beat was already history, the guidance changes the future. (expectations)
4. **Why was guidance lower?** Management said one large cloud customer is pausing orders while it
   switches to a new server design. [call transcript]
5. **Why would a design change pause orders?** Customers stop buying parts for the old design
   before the new one is ready; orders resume when the new design ships. (demand timing,
   medium confidence: inferred from management's comment)

**Bottom line written from the ladder:** "Credo fell because its forecast for next quarter came in
low; management blames one big customer pausing orders during a server redesign. If that is
right, the orders are delayed rather than lost, and next quarter's report is the test."

## Rules
- Do not skip rungs: each "because" must be visible.
- Mark each rung: sourced fact, or inference with confidence (high / medium / low).
- If two explanations compete, show both and say which evidence would decide.
- Never invent a rung to make the story neat. "No reason was published" is a valid rung.
