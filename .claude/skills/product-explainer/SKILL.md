# Product explainer

Santi wants to know what each product does, why anyone buys it, and how much it sells.
Announcements are promises; shipments are revenue. Make that distinction every time.

## The product card (fill every line: `references/product-card.md`)

1. **What it is**, one sentence, then an everyday analogy.
2. **What problem it solves, and for whom** (the actual buyers: cloud companies, enterprises, governments).
3. **How it works**, one step more precise than the analogy.
4. **Price**: per unit or per subscription, cited, or "not disclosed".
5. **How much it sells**: units, revenue, backlog, design wins or customer counts, cited. If not
   disclosed, say so, then give the best labelled proxy (segment revenue, management's comment,
   a cited industry estimate).
6. **Stage**: announced, sampling to customers, qualified, shipping, ramping in volume.
7. **Size for the company**: compare with the company's quarterly revenue ("if it reached $X a
   year, that would be about Y% of current sales").
8. **Competition**: who else sells something similar, and how this differs.
9. **Why investors care, and what to watch** (the next proof point).

## Where the facts come from

The company's press release and product page (tier 1) for what it is; 10-Q/10-K business and
segment sections, investor presentations and call transcripts for sales; tier-2 reporting for
customer adoption and competitive context. Never estimate sales from memory.

## Background knowledge

`references/technology-primers.md` has plain-English primers for the technologies behind the
current holdings (AI chips and data centers, networking, optics, copper connectivity,
cybersecurity platforms, site development). Use them for the "how it works" layer and update
them when a deep dive teaches something new; company-specific numbers never go in the primers.
