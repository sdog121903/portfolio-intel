# SEC filings in plain English

Official source for the definitions below: the SEC's form instructions at
https://www.sec.gov/forms (Form 8-K general instructions list the items).

## The forms

| Form | Plain English | Usually matters? |
|---|---|---|
| **10-K** | The annual report: full-year audited financials, business description, risk factors, management's discussion | High: read once a year in the deep dive |
| **10-Q** | The quarterly report: unaudited financials for the quarter, updated risks and discussion | High after earnings |
| **8-K** | "Something important happened": must be filed within four business days of the event | Depends on the items (below) |
| **Form 4** | An insider (director, officer, 10% owner) bought, sold or received shares; due within two business days | Sometimes (see codes) |
| **Form 144** | Notice that an insider plans to sell restricted or control shares | Low-medium: a plan, not a sale |
| **Form 3** | A new insider's starting holdings | Low |
| **S-3 / 424B** | Registration and prospectus to sell new shares or debt to the public | High if the company itself sells shares (dilution) |
| **S-8** | Registers shares for employee stock plans | Low (but it is ongoing dilution) |
| **13D** | Someone bought over 5% and may try to influence the company | High |
| **13G** | Someone owns over 5% passively (index funds, large investors) | Low |
| **DEF 14A** | Proxy statement: board elections, executive pay, shareholder votes | Medium once a year |
| **20-F / 6-K** | Annual / current reports of foreign companies | As 10-K / 8-K |

## Form 4 transaction codes

| Code | Meaning | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| P | Open-market or private purchase | Strongest signal: own money at market price |
| S | Open-market or private sale | Meaningful if unplanned and clustered; weak if under a 10b5-1 plan |
| A | Grant or award from the company | Routine pay |
| M | Exercise or conversion of options or other derivatives | Routine; check if followed by S |
| F | Shares withheld to pay tax or the exercise price | Routine |
| G | Gift | Routine |
| D | Shares returned to the company | Routine |
| X / C | Option exercise / conversion | Routine |
| J | Other (read the footnote) | Read it |

## Reading a 10-Q or 10-K quickly

1. **Revenue and margins** (Financial statements): compare with the same quarter last year.
2. **Segment data**: which part of the business drove growth.
3. **Management's discussion (MD&A)**: management's own explanation of why numbers moved:
   the best source for "why".
4. **Risk factors**: new or deleted risks versus the previous filing.
5. **Customer concentration**: notes often say "Customer A accounted for X% of revenue".
6. **Liquidity**: cash, debt, and any new share sales or buybacks.
7. **Legal proceedings**: new lawsuits or investigations.
