# Metrics that matter, by industry (for the current holdings)

Use these to decide what to look at first and to explain why it matters. Always cite the
company's own figure; if the company does not report a metric, say so.

## Semiconductors and AI hardware (AMD, TSM, CRDO, LITE)
- **Data-center revenue / segment revenue**: how much comes from AI data centers, the main growth driver.
- **Revenue growth year over year and quarter over quarter**: chips are cyclical; slowing
  quarter-over-quarter growth is often the first sign of a turn.
- **Gross margin**: pricing power. Falling margins can mean competition or a costly new product ramp.
- **Guidance**: management sees orders months ahead, so its forecast carries information.
- **Customer concentration**: share of revenue from the largest customers (often a few cloud
  companies). High concentration = big swings if one customer changes plans.
- **Inventory** (days of inventory, inventory growth vs sales): rising inventory with flat sales can
  mean demand is cooling.
- **Product transitions**: the move to the next speed or generation; who is qualified to supply it.
- **For a contract manufacturer (TSM)**: revenue by manufacturing process (newest vs older), wafer
  shipments, capacity use and capital spending; TSMC also publishes revenue every month.

## Cybersecurity and data software (CRWD, PANW, SNOW)
- **ARR (annual recurring revenue)** and **net new ARR**: the yearly value of subscriptions and how
  much was added this quarter; the most watched number.
- **Net revenue retention**: whether existing customers spend more each year (above 100% = they do).
- **Remaining performance obligations (RPO)**: contracted revenue not yet recognised.
- **Free cash flow margin**: software companies often show low GAAP profit but strong cash flow.
- **Stock-based compensation**: a real cost that dilutes owners; watch it relative to revenue.
- **GAAP profitability**: whether the company makes a profit under official rules.
- **Usage-based software (SNOW)**: product revenue (customers pay for computing used), so watch
  consumption trends and the company's product-revenue guidance more than seat counts.

## Infrastructure construction (STRL)
- **Backlog** (signed work not yet done) and **book-to-bill** (new orders / revenue): above 1 means
  the backlog is growing.
- **Segment revenue and margins**: data-center site work vs transportation vs building solutions.
- **Mix**: higher-margin site development vs lower-margin public projects.
- **Project timing**: big projects can shift revenue between quarters.
- **Customer concentration**: a few very large data-center developers.

## Medicines and vaccines (MRNA)
- **Product sales by product**: which approved products sell, and how seasonal they are.
- **Cash and runway**: cash on hand against the yearly cash burn; how long until more money is needed.
- **Pipeline milestones**: trial results, regulator filings and decisions, with dates.
- **Operating costs against the company's own targets** (research spending, cost cuts).

## Lab instruments and tools (A)
- **Organic revenue growth** (excluding acquisitions and currency).
- **Orders by customer group** (drug makers, chemicals, food and environment, diagnostics).
- **Recurring share**: consumables and service contracts, the steadier part of revenue.
- **Operating margin** and the yearly guidance.

## Power equipment (BE)
- **Product revenue and systems shipped** (or "not disclosed"), plus service revenue.
- **Backlog and new orders**, especially from data centers and utilities.
- **Gross margin** and **cash flow from operations**: whether growth is becoming profitable.
- **Guidance** for the year.
