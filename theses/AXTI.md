## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
AXT makes the thin, flat discs ("wafers" or "substrates") that lasers and other special chips are built on, mostly from a material called indium phosphide (InP), in factories mainly in China. AI data centers use InP lasers to send data as light, so demand for these wafers has soared: revenue rose from $18.0 million to $47.6 million in a year (research file, call transcript). The catch: China must issue export permits before the wafers can ship, and permits for US customers are still not issued [AXT 10-Q, 2026-08-13].

## How it makes money
- Indium phosphide (InP) wafers for lasers and optical chips: $30.7m of the $47.6m quarter (research file, call transcript).
- Other products make up the rest (the split is not in our files; the deep dive will add it).
- Customers can prepay to reserve capacity: Lumentum paid two $43.5m deposits under a six-year deal [AXT 8-K, 2026-07-29].

### Thesis 1: "AXT is one of the few companies that can make indium phosphide wafers at scale, and AI lasers need them."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Revenue rose about 2.6 times in a year, to $47.6m, and Q3 guidance of about $66m would be another record (call transcript, tier 3). Few other suppliers make InP wafers at scale (inference, medium confidence).
2. **What must stay true?** Revenue keeps rising, and China keeps granting enough export permits for AXT to ship.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - China stops or further restricts permits.
   - A rival ramps up large-scale InP wafer production.
   - Revenue falls from one quarter to the next.

### Thesis 2: "Big customers pay in advance to secure AXT's wafers, which shows how scarce they are."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Lumentum, another of your holdings, signed a six-year deal reserving AXT's production with two $43.5m deposits [AXT 8-K, 2026-07-29].
2. **What must stay true?** The Lumentum deal holds, and other customers sign similar prepaid deals.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - The Lumentum deal is cancelled or cut back.
   - Customers announce other wafer suppliers instead.

**The other side (reasons for caution):** China controls the permits, and US customers still cannot get them. In April it sold new shares (about 15% more), and in June the CEO and three directors sold without pre-planned plans. It is your most volatile stock: a typical day moves it 8.3%, and it fell 74% from May to July (data/metrics).
