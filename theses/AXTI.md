## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold AXT because AI lasers are built on indium phosphide wafers, few companies can make them at scale, and customers are prepaying to get them.

AI data centers send data as light, and the lasers that make that light are built on indium phosphide (InP) wafers. AXT is one of the few companies that can make those wafers at scale (inference, medium confidence), so it sits at a narrow point in the supply chain: revenue rose about 2.6 times in a year, from $18.0m to $47.6m, with Q3 guided to about $66m (research file; call transcript, tier 3). The clearest sign of scarcity is that customers pay in advance to secure supply: Lumentum, another of my holdings, signed a six-year deal backed by two $43.5m deposits [AXT 8-K, 2026-07-29].

**What I accept (the risks):** this is my most volatile stock (it fell 74% from May to July), its factories are mainly in China and China has not issued export permits for US customers, which AXT calls its most significant challenge [AXT 10-Q, 2026-08-13]; an April share sale added about 15% more shares; and the CEO and three directors sold outside pre-planned plans in June.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
AXT makes the thin, flat discs ("wafers" or "substrates") that lasers and other special chips are built on, mostly from a material called indium phosphide (InP), in factories mainly in China. AI data centers use InP lasers to send data as light, so demand for these wafers has soared: revenue rose from $18.0 million to $47.6 million in a year (research file, call transcript). The catch: China must issue export permits before the wafers can ship, and permits for US customers are still not issued [AXT 10-Q, 2026-08-13].

## How it makes money
- Indium phosphide (InP) wafers for lasers and optical chips: $30.7m of the $47.6m quarter (research file, call transcript).
- Other products make up the rest (the split is not in our files; the deep dive will add it).
- Customers can prepay to reserve capacity: Lumentum paid two $43.5m deposits under a six-year deal [AXT 8-K, 2026-07-29].

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)

1. **Revenue keeps rising.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Rising revenue is the evidence that AI laser demand is reaching AXT's wafers.
   - *Measured by:* Quarterly revenue against guidance (Q3: about $66m, tier 3) and the previous quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $47.6m last quarter, up from $18.0m a year earlier (research file).
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, 2026-10-29 after the close (confirmed).
2. **China grants enough export permits for AXT to ship.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Most production is in China; without permits the wafers cannot reach customers abroad.
   - *Measured by:* What the 10-Q and calls say about permits granted, especially for US customers.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Permits for US customers still not issued [AXT 10-Q, 2026-08-13].
   - *Next check:* Q3 results 2026-10-29 and the next 10-Q.
3. **InP wafers stay scarce, with few large rivals.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Scarcity is what lets AXT win prepaid deals; new large rivals would end it.
   - *Measured by:* News of rivals adding large InP wafer capacity.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Few other suppliers at scale (inference, medium confidence).
   - *Next check:* News in every daily report.
4. **The Lumentum deal holds, and more customers prepay.** *(Thesis 2)*

   - *Measured by:* Customer deposits and supply agreements in 8-Ks and 10-Qs.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Lumentum: six-year deal, two $43.5m deposits [AXT 8-K, 2026-07-29].
