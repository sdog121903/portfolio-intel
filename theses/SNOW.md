## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Snowflake because it charges for the computing customers use on their data, and AI makes them use much more of it.

Snowflake stores companies' data in the cloud and runs their analysis on it, billing like a taxi meter for the computing used. AI work on company data needs a lot of computing, so AI raises Snowflake's revenue directly: product revenue growth sped up for three quarters in a row, to 37% ($1,491.9m), and the company says its new AI tools drove about half of the speed-up [Snowflake Q2 FY2027 results, 2026-09-02; research file]. Customers who join keep spending more: existing customers spent 26% more than a year earlier (net revenue retention 126%). It has also just raised $3.75bn with 0% bonds, so it has money to invest without paying interest, with a hedge that limits dilution [SEC 8-K, 2026-10-02].

**What I accept (the risks):** it still loses money under standard accounting ($191.7m last quarter), contracted future revenue slipped from $9.21bn to $9.00bn, a shareholder lawsuit is pending, and the stock is up about 188% from its April low.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Snowflake stores companies' data in the cloud and lets them analyse it, for example to see sales by region or to feed AI tools. Customers pay for the computing they use, like a taxi meter, so the more work they do on their data (including AI work), the more Snowflake bills. Product revenue was $1,491.9 million last quarter, up 37%, and existing customers spent 26% more than a year earlier (net revenue retention of 126%) [Snowflake Q2 FY2027 results, 2026-09-02].

## How it makes money
Mostly usage-based product revenue (customers pay for the computing they consume), plus some
services. The deep dive will add the split, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Product revenue growth
- Net revenue retention (do existing customers spend more each year?)
- Remaining performance obligation (contracted future revenue)
- Free cash flow margin and stock-based compensation
- Guidance for product revenue

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)
Each pillar says why it matters, how it is measured, where it stands now and when it is next checked. The daily report tests every one; when a "Now" line changes, the change log records it.

1. **Product revenue keeps growing near its recent pace.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Usage-based revenue shows in real time whether customers are doing more on Snowflake.
   - *Measured by:* Product revenue growth against a year earlier; next-quarter guidance $1,588-1,593m.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $1,491.9m, +37%, third quarter of faster growth [Snowflake Q2 FY2027 results].
   - *Next check:* Q3 FY2027 results, 2026-12-02 (not confirmed).
2. **AI tools keep adding to growth.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* AI is the reason for the speed-up; if AI usage stalls, growth goes back to its old, slower pace.
   - *Measured by:* What management says about AI products' share of growth and AI customer counts.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* AI tools drove about half of the speed-up (company, per research file).
   - *Next check:* Q3 FY2027 earnings call.
3. **Net revenue retention stays well above 100%.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Above 100% means existing customers alone grow revenue each year.
   - *Measured by:* Net revenue retention in each results release.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* 126% [Snowflake Q2 FY2027 results].
   - *Next check:* Q3 FY2027 results.
4. **Contracted future revenue grows again.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* RPO (remaining performance obligations: contracts signed but not yet billed) is future revenue already committed.
   - *Measured by:* RPO against the previous quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $9.00bn, down from $9.21bn (research file, from company releases).
   - *Next check:* Q3 FY2027 results.
5. **The shareholder lawsuit does not reveal new problems.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* A court finding that investors were misled would damage trust and could cost money.
   - *Measured by:* Court rulings on the motions to dismiss; anything the 10-Q adds.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Motion to dismiss due 2026-10-13 in the case filed 2026-02-24 [Snowflake 10-Q].
   - *Next check:* 2026-10-13 and later court news.

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **Product revenue guidance is cut.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Usage-based guidance reflects what customers are actually doing; a cut means usage slowed.
   - *Counts if:* Next-quarter or full-year product revenue guidance below the previous range, or a quarter below its range (Q3: $1,588m).
   - *Early warning:* Guidance raised less than usual, or management warns of usage slowing.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next 2026-12-02.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **Growth slows sharply for two quarters in a row.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Two sharp slowdowns would mean the AI boost has faded.
   - *Counts if:* Product revenue growth below 30% (from 37%) for two quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* Growth below 33% in one quarter.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **Net revenue retention falls toward 100% (existing customers stop growing their spending).** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* At 100%, existing customers stop adding to growth; only new customers would.
   - *Counts if:* Net revenue retention at 115% or below.
   - *Early warning:* Two quarters in a row of falling retention.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **Contracted future revenue keeps shrinking quarter after quarter.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Shrinking commitments mean customers are signing smaller or shorter contracts.
   - *Counts if:* RPO lower than the previous quarter for two more quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* A second quarter of lower RPO.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-05):* Not hit. The early warning (a second quarter of lower RPO) is not present yet: RPO fell once ($9.21bn to $9.00bn), which weakens pillar 4.
5. **Snowflake sells new shares or more convertibles adding more than 5%.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* On top of the 2.2-4% possible from the new bonds, more dilution would weaken the per-share case.
   - *Counts if:* A share sale or new convertible issue that could add more than 5% of shares.
   - *Early warning:* A new shelf registration or financing announcement.
   - *Where and when to check:* SEC filings in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
