## What the company does (plain English)
TSMC manufactures chips that other companies design: it is the factory behind most of the
world's most advanced processors, including those from Apple, NVIDIA and AMD. It does not sell
chips under its own name; customers pay it per wafer (a disc of silicon holding many chips).
In the US it trades as an ADR, a US-listed certificate that stands for shares listed in Taiwan.

## How it makes money
Wafer manufacturing for customers, priced higher for the newest, smallest manufacturing
processes. Revenue by end market (smartphones, high-performance computing, cars) and by process is
in its own quarterly reports, which the deep dive will cite.

## Metrics that matter
- Monthly revenue (TSMC publishes it every month)
- Gross margin against its own guidance
- Share of revenue from the most advanced processes
- Capital spending plans
- Guidance for the next quarter

## What must stay true (proposed starting points: edit or delete)
1. Demand for AI and high-end chips keeps TSMC's advanced factories full.
2. It stays ahead of rival chip factories on the most advanced manufacturing.

## Invalidation triggers (proposed starting points: edit or delete)
1. Gross margin falls well below guidance for two quarters without a stated one-off cause.
2. A major customer moves its leading chips to a rival factory, or trade rules cut off key customers.
