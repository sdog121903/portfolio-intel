## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
TSMC is a contract chipmaker (a "foundry"): companies like NVIDIA, AMD and Apple design chips, and TSMC manufactures them in its factories, mostly in Taiwan. It is like a printing house that prints other people's books, except each printing press costs billions of dollars, so few rivals can keep up. It earns the most on the newest, smallest chip designs; last quarter revenue was US$40.20 billion (+36%) with a 67.7% gross margin [TSMC Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-16].

## How it makes money
Wafer manufacturing for customers, priced higher for the newest, smallest manufacturing
processes. Revenue by end market (smartphones, high-performance computing, cars) and by process is
in its own quarterly reports, which the deep dive will cite.

### Thesis 1: "TSMC is the factory behind the AI chips of NVIDIA and AMD, so AI demand shows up directly in its sales."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** August sales were a record, up 53.3% from a year earlier, driven by AI chips [TSMC, 2026-09-10]; Q2 revenue grew 36% [TSMC Q2 2026 results].
2. **What must stay true?** Monthly sales keep growing from a year earlier, and quarterly results land inside guidance.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - Monthly sales fall from a year earlier for several months in a row.
   - A major customer moves its advanced chips to another manufacturer.

### Thesis 2: "Its lead in advanced manufacturing lets it charge high prices."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** A 67.7% gross margin is very high for a manufacturer, a sign customers pay up for the newest processes [TSMC Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-16].
2. **What must stay true?** Gross margin stays inside the company's guided range (65-67% for Q3).
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - Gross margin falls below guidance.
   - A rival (for example Samsung or Intel) wins a major customer's most advanced chips.

**The other side (reasons for caution):** Q3 margin is guided lower while new 2-nanometre factories ramp up. Factory spending is heavy, and a Texas campus would add more. A US decision on chip tariffs is pending (tier-3 reports only). Most of its factories are in Taiwan, so Taiwan's political situation is a risk that owning other stocks cannot remove from this one (inference, high confidence).
