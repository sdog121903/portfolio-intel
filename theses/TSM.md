## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold TSMC because it is the factory that actually manufactures the AI chips other companies design, so AI demand shows up directly in its sales.

NVIDIA, AMD, Apple and many others design chips but do not make them; TSMC does, in factories that cost billions of dollars each and that few rivals can match. That makes it a way to own AI chip demand without having to guess which designer wins, since the leading designers use it (inference, high confidence): August sales were a record NT$514.81bn, up 53.3% from a year earlier, driven by AI chips [TSMC, 2026-09-10]. Its lead in the newest, smallest manufacturing processes lets it charge high prices, with a 67.7% gross margin last quarter on US$40.20bn of revenue (+36%) [TSMC Q2 2026 results]. It is also one of my calmer holdings (a typical day moves it about 2.2%), which balances the jumpier stocks.

**What I accept (the risks):** most of its factories are in Taiwan, a political risk that owning other stocks cannot remove; new 2-nanometre factories are lowering the margin for now (Q3 guided 65-67%); factory spending is heavy; and US chip tariffs are still undecided.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
TSMC is a contract chipmaker (a "foundry"): companies like NVIDIA, AMD and Apple design chips, and TSMC manufactures them in its factories, mostly in Taiwan. It is like a printing house that prints other people's books, except each printing press costs billions of dollars, so few rivals can keep up. It earns the most on the newest, smallest chip designs; last quarter revenue was US$40.20 billion (+36%) with a 67.7% gross margin [TSMC Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-16].

## How it makes money
Wafer manufacturing for customers, priced higher for the newest, smallest manufacturing
processes. Revenue by end market (smartphones, high-performance computing, cars) and by process is
in its own quarterly reports, which the deep dive will cite.

## Metrics that matter
- Monthly revenue (TSMC publishes it every month)
- Gross margin against its own guidance
- Share of revenue from the most advanced processes
- Capital spending plans
- Guidance for the next quarter

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **Monthly sales fall from a year earlier for several months in a row.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* It would mean AI chip demand is cooling at the source.
   - *Counts if:* Monthly revenue below the same month a year earlier for three months in a row.
   - *Early warning:* One month below a year earlier, or two months of falling sales.
   - *Where and when to check:* Monthly sales releases (around the 10th of each month).
   - *Status now (2026-10-09):* Not hit. September +54.6% from a year earlier; September's −0.6% from August is the first month-on-month dip (the early warning needs two) [TSMC 6-K, 2026-10-08](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046179/000104617926000680/tsm-revenue20261008.htm).
2. **A major customer moves its advanced chips to another manufacturer.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* TSMC's growth depends on a handful of very large designers.
   - *Counts if:* NVIDIA, AMD, Apple or another top customer announces its newest chip will be made mainly elsewhere.
   - *Early warning:* A top customer signs a large test agreement with Samsung or Intel.
   - *Where and when to check:* News in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **Gross margin falls below guidance.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Missing its own margin range would mean either weaker pricing or cost problems in new factories.
   - *Counts if:* Quarterly gross margin below the bottom of the guided range (Q3: 65%).
   - *Early warning:* Next-quarter margin guidance below 63%.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next 2026-10-15.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **A rival (for example Samsung or Intel) wins a major customer's most advanced chips.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* It would mean the manufacturing lead is shrinking.
   - *Counts if:* A tier-1/2 source confirms a top customer's leading-edge chip in volume production at a rival.
   - *Early warning:* Reports that a rival's newest process has matched TSMC's yields (the share of good chips per wafer).
   - *Where and when to check:* News in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **A Taiwan conflict, blockade or sanctions disrupt TSMC's operations or exports.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* This is the one risk that could hit the business overnight and that diversification does not remove.
   - *Counts if:* Official or tier-1/2 reports that TSMC's Taiwan production or shipping is disrupted.
   - *Early warning:* A major escalation around Taiwan, such as exercises that disrupt shipping.
   - *Where and when to check:* Every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
6. **US chip tariffs cut TSMC's sales or margin.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* A tariff could make its chips more expensive for US customers or force costly changes.
   - *Counts if:* A tariff takes effect and TSMC lowers guidance or says it cuts its margin.
   - *Early warning:* A tariff is formally announced with a start date.
   - *Where and when to check:* News (tier-3 reports only so far); TSMC results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-09):* Not hit. Secondary sources (not confirmed) say a narrow 25% Section 232 chip tariff has applied since 2026-01-15 with an exemption framework for Taiwan, so "a decision is pending" may be out of date; to verify with a tier-1 source.
