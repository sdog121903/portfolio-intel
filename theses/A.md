## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Agilent as the steadier, different stock in a mostly-tech portfolio, backed by a business whose orders are growing.

Most of my money rides on chips and software; Agilent is a lab-instrument company, so it adds something that does not move with them: it swings less than the market (beta 0.84) and almost independently of my chip stocks (60-day correlations near zero) (data/metrics). That fits my own goal of diversifying beyond tech. The business is also improving: labs are replacing ageing instruments and drug makers, especially for weight-loss (GLP-1) drugs, are spending more, so customer orders have exceeded shipments for 10 quarters (research file, company call), and the last two results beat forecasts and raised the yearly outlook [Agilent Q3 FY2026 results, 2026-08-26]. Each instrument sold keeps bringing in supplies and service for years, like a printer and its ink.

**What I accept (the risks):** growth is modest (+7.3% core), UBS lowered its rating with a $165 target, and $0.06 of last quarter's profit per share was a one-time tariff refund, so the price depends on steady execution.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Agilent makes laboratory instruments, such as the machines drug companies use to check exactly what is in a sample, plus the supplies and servicing those machines need. Once a lab buys an instrument it keeps buying supplies and service for years, like a printer and its ink. Right now drug makers are replacing ageing machines and spending more on weight-loss (GLP-1) drugs, and revenue was $1.88 billion last quarter [Agilent Q3 FY2026 results, 2026-08-26; research file].

## How it makes money
Instrument sales plus a steady stream of consumables and service contracts (the 'razor and
blades' pattern: sell the machine once, then sell what it uses up). The deep dive will add the
split by segment and customer type, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Organic revenue growth (excluding acquisitions and currency effects)
- Orders from pharmaceutical and biotech customers
- Operating margin
- Share of revenue that recurs (consumables and services)
- Guidance for the year

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)

   - *Measured by:* Book-to-bill (orders divided by shipments) as described by management each quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Above 1 for 10 quarters (company call, per research file).
   - *Next check:* Q4 FY2026 results, late November (not confirmed).
2. **The yearly forecast is kept or raised.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The forecast was raised twice; keeping it confirms the lab spending recovery.
   - *Measured by:* Full-year revenue and profit guidance.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Raised with Q3 results; Q3 revenue $1.88bn, adjusted EPS $1.62, both above forecasts [Agilent Q3 FY2026 results].
   - *Next check:* Late November.
3. **It keeps moving differently from my tech holdings.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* This is the diversification reason; if it starts moving with chips, it no longer does that job.
   - *Measured by:* 60-day correlation with my chip holdings (data/metrics/portfolio).
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Near zero.
   - *Next check:* Every daily report.
4. **It stays calmer than the market.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* A calm holding lowers the swings of the whole portfolio.
   - *Measured by:* Beta (how much it moves with the market) and typical daily move (data/metrics).
