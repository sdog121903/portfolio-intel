## What the company does (plain English)
Agilent sells laboratory instruments, the supplies they use up, software and services. Drug
makers, hospitals, food and environmental testers and research labs use them to measure what is
in a sample.

## How it makes money
Instrument sales plus a steady stream of consumables and service contracts (the 'razor and
blades' pattern: sell the machine once, then sell what it uses up). The deep dive will add the
split by segment and customer type, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Organic revenue growth (excluding acquisitions and currency effects)
- Orders from pharmaceutical and biotech customers
- Operating margin
- Share of revenue that recurs (consumables and services)
- Guidance for the year

## What must stay true (proposed starting points: edit or delete)
1. Labs keep using and replacing Agilent instruments, so recurring revenue keeps growing.
2. Pharmaceutical customers' spending on equipment recovers or holds up.

## Invalidation triggers (proposed starting points: edit or delete)
1. Organic revenue declines for two quarters in a row without a stated one-off cause.
2. The company cuts its yearly guidance because major customers pull back.
