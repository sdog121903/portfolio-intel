## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Agilent makes laboratory instruments, such as the machines drug companies use to check exactly what is in a sample, plus the supplies and servicing those machines need. Once a lab buys an instrument it keeps buying supplies and service for years, like a printer and its ink. Right now drug makers are replacing ageing machines and spending more on weight-loss (GLP-1) drugs, and revenue was $1.88 billion last quarter [Agilent Q3 FY2026 results, 2026-08-26; research file].

## How it makes money
Instrument sales plus a steady stream of consumables and service contracts (the 'razor and
blades' pattern: sell the machine once, then sell what it uses up). The deep dive will add the
split by segment and customer type, with sources.

### Thesis 1: "Labs are replacing old instruments and drug makers are spending more, so Agilent's orders keep growing."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Two results in a row beat forecasts and raised the yearly outlook [Agilent Q3 FY2026 results]; customer orders have exceeded shipments for 10 quarters and weight-loss-drug work grew 70% or more (research file, company call).
2. **What must stay true?** Orders keep exceeding shipments, and the yearly forecast is kept or raised.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - The yearly forecast is cut.
   - Orders fall below shipments (a shrinking pipeline of work).

### Thesis 2: "Agilent is the steadier, different stock in a tech-heavy portfolio."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** It moves less than the market (beta 0.84) and almost independently of your chip stocks (60-day correlations near zero) (data/metrics).
2. **What must stay true?** It keeps moving differently from your tech holdings.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - Its correlation with your chip stocks rises a lot.
   - It starts swinging like a tech stock (much larger daily moves).
