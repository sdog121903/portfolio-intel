## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Lumentum because AI data centers connect their chips with laser light, Lumentum makes the lasers, and demand is bigger than it can ship.

As AI data centers grow, thousands of chips must exchange data, and over any distance that is done with laser light through glass fibre. Lumentum makes the lasers and optical parts that turn electrical signals into light, so it grows with the number and speed of those links: revenue more than doubled to $1.01bn last quarter (+109%), and the next quarter is guided to $1.225-1.275bn [Lumentum Q4 FY2026 results, 2026-08-11]. Management says it is still shipping behind customer demand, which usually means pricing power, and it has locked in a scarce input with a six-year wafer deal at AXT (another of my holdings) [AXT 8-K, 2026-07-29]. A possible US push to keep Chinese optical parts out of sensitive networks could add to that, though it is not law.

**What I accept (the risks):** the stock is very jumpy (it lost 42.8% at its worst point in the past year), two customers are 41.6% of revenue, a bond swap added about 13% more shares, and the price is about 31 times yearly sales (tier-3 estimate).

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Inside AI data centers, thousands of chips must exchange data, and over longer distances that is done with laser light travelling through glass fibre. Lumentum makes the lasers and optical parts that turn electrical signals into light, plus switches that steer light between fibres. Its buyers are module makers and cloud giants (two customers were 41.6% of last year's revenue), and last quarter its revenue more than doubled to $1.01 billion [Lumentum Q4 FY2026 results, 2026-08-11].

## How it makes money
- Lasers and optical components, mainly for cloud and AI data centers (other markets and the split: the deep dive will add them).
- Optical modules and optical circuit switches that steer light (unit sales not disclosed).
- Two customers were 41.6% of last fiscal year's revenue (10-K, per research file).

## Metrics that matter
- Revenue growth and guidance, especially from cloud and AI data-center customers
- Gross margin
- Capacity to meet demand (supply constraints can cap sales)
- Customer concentration

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **Revenue guidance is cut.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* It would mean AI laser demand has peaked or a big customer has paused.
   - *Counts if:* A quarter's revenue below its guided range, or next-quarter guidance below the current quarter's actual revenue.
   - *Early warning:* Guidance only at the same level as the current quarter (growth stalls).
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next early November.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **Management says supply has caught up with demand and prices are falling.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The end of the shortage usually means falling prices and margins for component makers.
   - *Counts if:* Management states supply now meets or exceeds demand, or that average selling prices are falling.
   - *Early warning:* Shorter lead times or customers building inventory, mentioned on calls.
   - *Where and when to check:* Earnings calls.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **One of its two biggest customers cuts orders.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* With 41.6% of revenue in two customers, one cut can hit results hard.
   - *Counts if:* The company or a tier-1/2 source reports a top customer cutting or delaying orders.
   - *Early warning:* A big customer qualifies a second laser supplier.
   - *Where and when to check:* News in every daily report; 10-Q customer note.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **The bill dies or is watered down.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Then the policy tailwind disappears (this is the speculative part of my case).
   - *Counts if:* The bill fails in committee or the session ends without it, or the final text no longer covers transceivers.
   - *Early warning:* No movement in committee for six months.
   - *Where and when to check:* US Congress record.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **Lumentum reports no change in orders from US customers.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* If the rules pass but orders do not move, the reason did not work.
   - *Counts if:* Two quarters after any rule takes effect, management reports no shift in US-customer demand.
   - *Early warning:* Management declines to quantify any benefit.
   - *Where and when to check:* Earnings calls.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
6. **A new share sale or convertible issue adds more than 5% to the share count.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* Dilution on top of the 13% already done would weaken the per-share case.
   - *Counts if:* A prospectus (424B) or convertible issue that could add more than 5% of shares.
   - *Early warning:* A new shelf registration (S-3) or a large convertible offering announced.
   - *Where and when to check:* SEC filings in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
