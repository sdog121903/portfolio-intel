## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Applied Optoelectronics because it is scaling up fast to supply the high-speed transceivers AI data centers need, and it has just turned profitable on an adjusted basis.

Every server link in an AI data center needs a transceiver, a plug-in module that turns electrical signals into laser light and back, and data centers are moving to the fastest ones (800 and 1,600 gigabits per second). AOI is building factories to make far more of them: capacity for about 650,000 high-speed units a month by end-2026, with record revenue of $191.9m (+86.4%) last quarter and Q3 guided to $255-290m [AOI Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-06]. It also reported its first adjusted profit, a sign that more volume is turning into earnings. It is a smaller, riskier way to ride the same optical boom as Lumentum.

**What I accept (the risks):** it pays for its factories partly by selling new shares (shares rose from 75.0m to 84.4m in six months, with up to $600m more possible), its top 10 customers are 99% of revenue and one distributor owes it $211.0m, it still loses money under standard accounting, and it fell 67% from May to July.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Applied Optoelectronics (AOI) makes optical transceivers: plug-in modules that turn a server's electrical signals into laser light and back, at 800 or 1,600 gigabits per second, like a translator at each end of a phone line. Data centers were 56.1% of its sales last quarter, and it also sells amplifiers for cable-TV networks. Revenue hit a record $191.9 million (+86.4%), and it is building factories to make far more modules, paid for partly by selling new shares [AOI Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-06].

## How it makes money
- Optical transceivers for data centers: 56.1% of Q2 2026 revenue [AOI Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-06].
- The rest (43.9%) comes from other markets, including cable-TV amplifiers (exact split not in our files).
- Its top 10 customers are 99% of revenue [AOI 10-Q, 2026-08-06].

## Metrics that matter
- Data-center revenue and its growth
- Factory capacity against the target of about 650,000 high-speed units a month by end-2026
- Quarterly revenue against guidance ($255-290m for Q3)
- Share count (new shares sold under its $600m program)
- Money owed by customers (receivables), especially the largest distributor

   - *Early warning:* Revenue at the bottom of the range, or customers delaying orders.
   - *Where and when to check:* Q3 results, estimated 2026-11-05.
