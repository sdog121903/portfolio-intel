## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Applied Optoelectronics (AOI) makes optical transceivers: plug-in modules that turn a server's electrical signals into laser light and back, at 800 or 1,600 gigabits per second, like a translator at each end of a phone line. Data centers were 56.1% of its sales last quarter, and it also sells amplifiers for cable-TV networks. Revenue hit a record $191.9 million (+86.4%), and it is building factories to make far more modules, paid for partly by selling new shares [AOI Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-06].

## How it makes money
- Optical transceivers for data centers: 56.1% of Q2 2026 revenue [AOI Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-06].
- The rest (43.9%) comes from other markets, including cable-TV amplifiers (exact split not in our files).
- Its top 10 customers are 99% of revenue [AOI 10-Q, 2026-08-06].

### Thesis 1: "AOI is scaling up to become a major supplier of the fastest data-center transceivers."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Q3 guidance of $255-290m is well above Q2's record $191.9m, and it is building capacity for about 650,000 high-speed units a month by end-2026 [AOI Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-06].
2. **What must stay true?** AOI meets its revenue guidance, and new factory capacity comes online on schedule.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - Revenue misses or guidance is cut.
   - Factory ramp-up is delayed.
   - A top customer cuts orders.

### Thesis 2: "AOI is turning profitable as its volumes grow."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** It reported its first adjusted profit in Q2 after five record quarters in a row [AOI Q2 2026 results].
2. **What must stay true?** Adjusted profit continues, and the standard (GAAP) loss narrows.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - It returns to an adjusted loss.
   - GAAP losses widen as revenue grows.

**The other side (reasons for caution):** Shares outstanding rose from 75.0m to 84.4m in six months, and up to $600m more may be sold. Its top 10 customers are 99% of revenue, and one distributor owes it $211.0m. It still loses money under standard accounting and fell 67% from May to July; it is still 50.5% below its May high.
