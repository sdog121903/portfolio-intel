## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Sterling because every new data center and big factory starts with ground work, and Sterling is one of the builders that does it.

Before a data center can be built, the land has to be cleared, levelled, given foundations and connected to water and power. Sterling does that work, so it gets paid early in the AI building boom, without depending on which chip or software company wins: revenue grew 90% to $1.168bn last quarter [Sterling Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-03]. Its signed backlog more than doubled to $4.33bn, about a year of revenue at its raised 2026 outlook of $4.00-4.15bn, so the next year is easier to see than for most companies. It also gives me a construction company instead of another chip or software company, although it still rides on data-center spending.

**What I accept (the risks):** it moves about three times as much as the market and fell 54% from its June high at its worst; the stock fell 13% after its last results, possibly because margins are not keeping up with revenue (inference); and a shelf filing lets it sell new shares whenever it chooses.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Sterling is a US construction company that prepares the ground for very large buildings: it clears and levels land, pours foundations and concrete, and lays utility lines. Most of its growth now comes from data centers and big factories, which need huge, well-prepared sites fast. It is paid per project, so its backlog (signed work not yet done), $4.33 billion and up 116%, shows how much revenue is already lined up [Sterling Q2 2026 results, 2026-08-03].

## How it makes money
Project revenue across its segments; the deep dive will add the split and the backlog, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Backlog and book-to-bill (new orders compared with revenue)
- Revenue and margin of the data-center-related segment
- Overall operating margin
- Concentration in a few very large customers

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)
Each pillar says why it matters, how it is measured, where it stands now and when it is next checked. The daily report tests every one; when a "Now" line changes, the change log records it.

1. **Backlog keeps growing.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Backlog is signed future revenue; growth means customers keep committing new projects.
   - *Measured by:* Backlog each quarter against the previous quarter and a year earlier.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $4.33bn, +116% from a year earlier [Sterling Q2 2026 results].
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, early November (not confirmed).
2. **The 2026 outlook of $4.00-4.15bn is met.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The outlook was raised in August; meeting it confirms the data-center work is turning into revenue.
   - *Measured by:* Quarterly revenue against the outlook; whether the range is kept, raised or cut.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Q2 revenue $1.168bn (+90%) [Sterling Q2 2026 results].
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, early November.
3. **New orders at least replace finished work.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* If new orders fall below the work completed, backlog shrinks and future revenue with it.
   - *Measured by:* Book-to-bill: new orders divided by revenue in the quarter (1 or above is healthy).
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Not in our files; backlog more than doubling implies well above 1 (inference).
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, early November.
4. **Profit margins keep up with revenue.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* Growing revenue at thinner margins is less valuable; the August sell-off may have been about this.
   - *Measured by:* Gross and operating margin against a year earlier.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Exact margins not in our files; stock fell 13% after hours on 2026-08-03 (cause inferred, medium confidence).
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, early November.
5. **No share sale under the shelf registration.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* The shelf lets it sell shares at any time; a sale would dilute owners.
   - *Measured by:* Prospectus (424B) filings under the May 2026 shelf.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* None sold so far [QuiverQuant, 2026-05-18, tier 3].
   - *Next check:* SEC filings in every daily report.

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **Backlog shrinks for two quarters in a row.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Shrinking backlog means future revenue is shrinking.
   - *Counts if:* Reported backlog below the previous quarter's, two quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* One quarter of lower backlog, or backlog growth from a year earlier below 25%.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next early November.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **The 2026 outlook is cut.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* A cut would mean projects are slipping or being lost.
   - *Counts if:* Full-year 2026 revenue outlook below $4.00bn.
   - *Early warning:* Q3 revenue below analysts' expectations, or the range narrowed to its low end.
   - *Where and when to check:* Q3 results, early November.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **Big tech companies announce cuts to data-center building plans.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Data centers are most of Sterling's growth; their capital spending is its order book.
   - *Counts if:* Two or more of the largest cloud companies cut their capital-spending plans in the same results season.
   - *Early warning:* One large cloud company delays or cancels data-center sites.
   - *Where and when to check:* Big tech results (late October) and news in daily reports.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **Customers cancel or delay large projects.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Backlog only becomes revenue if projects go ahead.
   - *Counts if:* Sterling reports cancellations or delays that cut backlog or revenue guidance.
   - *Early warning:* News of delays at sites where Sterling is known to work.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results and 10-Q.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **Book-to-bill stays below 1 for several quarters.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* It is the early version of a shrinking backlog.
   - *Counts if:* Book-to-bill below 1 for three quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* Book-to-bill below 1 in one quarter.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
6. **Sterling sells new shares under its shelf adding more than 5%.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* A large sale would dilute owners and suggest it needs money.
   - *Counts if:* A prospectus (424B) for a sale of more than 5% of the share count.
   - *Early warning:* Any prospectus supplement filed under the shelf.
   - *Where and when to check:* SEC filings in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
