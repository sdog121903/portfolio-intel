## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Credo makes active electrical cables (AECs): copper cables with a small chip inside each end that cleans up the signal so it can travel further, plus chips for optical links. They connect servers and network switches inside AI data centers, where thousands of chips must exchange data very fast. A few giant customers buy most of it: four customers were 84% of revenue (research file, from the 10-Q).

## How it makes money
Product sales (cables and chips) plus some licensing; the deep dive will add the split, with sources.

### Thesis 1: "AI data centers need many more fast connections, and Credo's cables are a leading way to link servers over short distances."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Revenue more than doubled (+114.7%) to $479.0m; next quarter is guided to $525-535m and full-year growth above 85% [Credo Q1 FY2027 results, 2026-09-01].
2. **What must stay true?** Credo meets its guidance, and its big customers keep using its cables in new data-center designs.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - Revenue guidance is cut.
   - A top customer switches to another technology or supplier.

### Thesis 2: "Credo can grow beyond copper cables into optical chips as data speeds rise."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** It completed the DustPhotonics purchase on 2026-05-27 [Credo via Business Wire, 2026-05-27].
2. **What must stay true?** Optical products start to show up in reported revenue.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - The optical business is reported as shrinking or losing customers.
   - A write-down of the DustPhotonics purchase.

### Thesis 3: "Credo can keep high profit margins as it grows."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Even after the drop, its standard gross margin was 64.5% last quarter [Credo Q1 FY2027 results].
2. **What must stay true?** Gross margin stays around its current level.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Gross margin falls further for two quarters in a row.

**The other side (reasons for caution):** Four customers are 84% of revenue, so losing one would hurt badly. The gross margin fell from 68.2% to 64.5%, and the stock dropped about 20% in a day despite beating forecasts. Insiders sold about $41.5m in late September (all pre-planned), and three banks cut their price targets.
