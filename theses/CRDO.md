## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Credo because AI data centers need many more fast, short connections between servers, and Credo's cables are a leading way to make them.

Inside an AI data center, servers and switches sit a few metres apart but must exchange enormous amounts of data. Credo's active electrical cables (copper cables with a chip in each end that cleans up the signal) are a cheaper, lower-power way to make those short links than optical links over a few metres (research file), so its sales grow with every AI cluster built: revenue more than doubled (+114.7%) to $479.0m last quarter, with the next quarter guided to $525-535m and full-year growth above 85% [Credo Q1 FY2027 results, 2026-09-01]. Its DustPhotonics purchase extends it into optical chips for when speeds and distances outgrow copper, and even after a drop its standard gross margin is still high at 64.5%; the adjusted margin, which leaves out acquisition-related costs, barely moved (68.3% to 68.0%), so the drop most likely reflects the purchase rather than weaker pricing (research file; inference, medium confidence).

**What I accept (the risks):** four customers are 84% of revenue, the margin fell from 68.2% and the stock dropped about 20% in a day despite beating forecasts, three banks cut their price targets, and insiders sold about $41.5m in late September (all pre-planned).

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Credo makes active electrical cables (AECs): copper cables with a small chip inside each end that cleans up the signal so it can travel further, plus chips for optical links. They connect servers and network switches inside AI data centers, where thousands of chips must exchange data very fast. A few giant customers buy most of it: four customers were 84% of revenue (research file, from the 10-Q).

## How it makes money
Product sales (cables and chips) plus some licensing; the deep dive will add the split, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Revenue growth and guidance
- Gross margin
- Customer concentration (how much comes from the largest one or two customers)
- Adoption of its cables and chips in the newest data-center designs

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)

1. **Revenue guidance is cut.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* It would mean AI cluster spending or Credo's share of it is slowing.
   - *Counts if:* A quarter below its guided range ($525m) or full-year growth guided below 85%.
   - *Early warning:* Revenue only at the bottom of the range.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next about 2026-12-02.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **A top customer switches to another technology or supplier.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* With 84% of revenue in four customers, one switch would hurt badly.
   - *Counts if:* The company or a tier-1/2 source reports a top customer moving its next design to a rival or to optics instead.
   - *Early warning:* A top customer's share falls by more than 10 points in a quarter without explanation.
   - *Where and when to check:* 10-Q; news in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **The optical business is reported as shrinking or losing customers.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Then the second growth leg is not working.
   - *Counts if:* Management reports lower optical revenue or lost optical customers.
   - *Early warning:* No optical figures given two quarters after the purchase.
   - *Where and when to check:* Earnings calls.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **A write-down of the DustPhotonics purchase.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* It would admit the optical bet is worth less than paid.
   - *Counts if:* An impairment charge tied to DustPhotonics in a 10-Q or 10-K.
   - *Early warning:* Key DustPhotonics staff leave or products are delayed.
   - *Where and when to check:* 10-Q and 10-K.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **Gross margin falls further for two quarters in a row.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* A falling margin would mean Credo's cables are becoming a commodity.
   - *Counts if:* GAAP gross margin lower than the previous quarter, two quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* Gross margin below 63% in any quarter.
