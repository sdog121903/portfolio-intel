## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Moderna makes mRNA medicines: shots that give the body temporary instructions to build a protein, which trains the immune system to fight a virus or a tumour. Today it mostly sells COVID vaccines and loses money (revenue $145 million and a net loss of $0.8 billion last quarter), and it now also has an approved mRNA flu shot [Moderna Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-31]. The big hope is a personalised cancer vaccine, made for each patient from their own tumour with partner Merck, which succeeded in a large final-stage trial in melanoma, a skin cancer.

## How it makes money
Mostly sales of its vaccines to governments, pharmacies and health systems, which rise and fall
with the respiratory-virus season; plus some partnership income. The deep dive will add the split
by product, with sources.

### Thesis 1: "Moderna's personalised cancer vaccine could become a large new business after its successful final-stage trial."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** The Phase 3 melanoma trial with Merck met its main goal; success at this last stage is rare, and the stock rose 177% that day [Moderna/Merck, 2026-08-19]. Moderna and Merck share costs and profits 50/50.
2. **What must stay true?** The full data on 2026-10-24 confirm a clear benefit with acceptable side effects, and regulators then approve the vaccine.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - The full data show a smaller benefit or safety problems.
   - Regulators reject or delay approval.
   - Merck pulls out of the partnership.

### Thesis 2: "mRNA is a platform: the same technology can produce many products, not just COVID shots."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** The FDA approved Moderna's mRNA flu shot, mFLUSIVA, for adults 50+ [NBC News, 2026-08-05], on top of the COVID vaccine and the cancer vaccine in development.
2. **What must stay true?** Sales of non-COVID products start to grow in the quarterly results.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - The flu shot sells poorly once sales are reported.
   - Further late-stage trial failures in other mRNA products.

### Thesis 3: "Moderna has enough cash to reach those launches without running out."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** It raised $3.0bn in September with convertible bonds [SEC 8-K, 2026-09-01], while losing $0.8bn last quarter [Moderna Q2 2026 results].
2. **What must stay true?** Cash covers spending until new products bring in meaningful sales.
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these:
   - Another large share or bond sale soon.
   - Quarterly losses grow faster than the company's plan.

**The other side (reasons for caution):** The price rests on future cancer sales while the company loses money today; it fell about 25% the day after its 177% jump. Citi sees $80 [CNBC, 2026-09-30]. The September bonds could add about 3.6% more shares. Very jumpy: a typical day moves it about 7.1% (data/metrics).
