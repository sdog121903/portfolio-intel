## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Moderna as a bet that its personalised cancer vaccine, which succeeded in a final-stage trial, becomes a large new business.

Moderna and Merck's personalised melanoma vaccine (intismeran, given with Merck's Keytruda) met its main goal in a large Phase 3 trial, the last round of patient testing before approval [Moderna/Merck, 2026-08-19]. Success at that stage is rare in cancer, and each vaccine is made for one patient from their own tumour's genetic code, so if it works it could be used across many cancers, not just one; Moderna and Merck share costs and profits 50/50. It is also a platform bet: the FDA has now approved Moderna's mRNA flu shot, mFLUSIVA, so the same technology produces several products [NBC News, 2026-08-05]. And it raised $3.0bn in September, which gives it cash to reach those launches [SEC 8-K, 2026-09-01]. This is the most speculative stock I own, and I treat it that way.

**What I accept (the risks):** today's business is small and loss-making (revenue $145m and a net loss of $0.8bn last quarter), the price already assumes large cancer sales (Citi sees $80), and the full data on 2026-10-24 could move the stock sharply in either direction.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Moderna makes mRNA medicines: shots that give the body temporary instructions to build a protein, which trains the immune system to fight a virus or a tumour. Today it mostly sells COVID vaccines and loses money (revenue $145 million and a net loss of $0.8 billion last quarter), and it now also has an approved mRNA flu shot [Moderna Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-31]. The big hope is a personalised cancer vaccine, made for each patient from their own tumour with partner Merck, which succeeded in a large final-stage trial in melanoma, a skin cancer.

## How it makes money
Mostly sales of its vaccines to governments, pharmacies and health systems, which rise and fall
with the respiratory-virus season; plus some partnership income. The deep dive will add the split
by product, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Product sales by vaccine, and how they change season to season
- Cash on hand and how fast it is being spent (runway)
- Late-stage trial results and regulator decisions (approvals, rejections, delays)
- Cost cuts against the company's own targets
- Guidance for the year's revenue

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)
Each pillar says why it matters, how it is measured, where it stands now and when it is next checked. The daily report tests every one; when a "Now" line changes, the change log records it.

1. **The full trial data confirm a clear benefit with acceptable side effects.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The whole cancer case rests on this trial; the full data show how big the benefit really is.
   - *Measured by:* The ESMO presentation: relapse-free and metastasis-free survival against Keytruda alone, and side-effect rates.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Topline result: met its main goals (relapse-free and distant-metastasis-free survival) [Moderna/Merck, 2026-08-19].
   - *Next check:* ESMO cancer conference, Presidential Symposium, 2026-10-24 at 10:30 a.m. New York time (abstract LBA1); company webcast 1:00 p.m. New York time [Moderna via ACCESS Newswire, 2026-09-21](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/moderna-announces-breaking-data-presented-141200784.html).
2. **Regulators approve the cancer vaccine.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Without approval there are no sales; approval timing decides when money starts to come in.
   - *Measured by:* Filing announcements, FDA acceptance, review dates and decisions.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Filing timing not yet announced (not in our files).
   - *Next check:* Company announcements; Q3 results (about 2026-10-29).
3. **The Merck partnership holds.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Merck shares the cost and brings Keytruda; losing it would make the project much harder and costlier.
   - *Measured by:* Any change in the 50/50 agreement disclosed in filings.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* In force; costs and profits shared 50/50.
   - *Next check:* Each 10-Q.
4. **Non-COVID sales start to grow.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Growth outside COVID is what proves mRNA is a platform, not a one-product company.
   - *Measured by:* Reported flu-vaccine and other non-COVID product sales each quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* mFLUSIVA approved for adults 50+ on 2026-08-05; sales not yet reported.
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, about 2026-10-29 (estimated).
5. **Cash lasts until new products bring in meaningful sales.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* A company that loses money must not run out of cash before its products launch.
   - *Measured by:* Cash on hand against the quarterly loss (how many quarters of spending the cash covers).
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Raised $3.0bn in September; Q2 net loss $0.8bn; total cash not in our files [SEC 8-K, 2026-09-01; Q2 results].
   - *Next check:* Q3 results, about 2026-10-29.

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **The full data show a smaller benefit or safety problems.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The price assumes a big cancer franchise; weak full data would remove the main reason I hold it.
   - *Counts if:* The ESMO data show a benefit the company or independent experts call modest, or serious side effects that limit use.
   - *Early warning:* Cautious comments from cancer specialists after the presentation, or a big share-price drop on the day.
   - *Where and when to check:* ESMO, 2026-10-24; news the same day.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **Regulators reject or delay approval.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Each delay pushes sales further away while the company keeps losing money.
   - *Counts if:* An FDA refusal, a request for another trial, or a review date pushed back.
   - *Early warning:* FDA advisory committee concerns, or a filing later than the company planned.
   - *Where and when to check:* Company announcements and 8-Ks.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **Merck pulls out of the partnership.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Merck knows the data best; leaving would be a strong negative signal and would double Moderna's costs.
   - *Counts if:* Merck ends or cuts its 50/50 participation.
   - *Early warning:* Merck renegotiates terms or sounds less committed on its own calls.
   - *Where and when to check:* 8-K filings; Merck's results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **The flu shot sells poorly once sales are reported.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* It is the first test of mRNA beyond COVID with real customers.
   - *Counts if:* In its first two reported seasons, flu-vaccine sales come in well below the company's own expectations, or it lowers its flu outlook.
   - *Early warning:* Low uptake reported by pharmacies or health systems.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results from Q3 2026.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **Further late-stage trial failures in other mRNA products.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Repeated failures would say the platform works less widely than hoped.
   - *Counts if:* A Phase 3 trial of another Moderna product misses its main goal.
   - *Early warning:* A trial stopped early or a key product delayed.
   - *Where and when to check:* Company announcements.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
6. **Another large share or bond sale soon.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Raising money again soon after $3.0bn would mean cash is burning faster than planned.
   - *Counts if:* A new share sale or convertible issue within 12 months of September 2026 that could add more than 3% of shares.
   - *Early warning:* A new shelf registration or a sharp rise in quarterly spending.
   - *Where and when to check:* SEC filings in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
7. **Quarterly losses grow faster than the company's plan.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Faster losses shorten the cash runway.
   - *Counts if:* The net loss is larger than a year earlier for two quarters in a row, or the company raises its spending outlook.
   - *Early warning:* One quarter worse than a year earlier.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
