## What the company does (plain English)
CrowdStrike sells cybersecurity software that runs on companies' laptops, servers and cloud
systems and watches for attacks. Customers pay yearly subscriptions and often add more modules
(cloud security, identity protection, security log analysis) over time.

## How it makes money
Mostly subscriptions; the deep dive will add the split by product and region, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Annual recurring revenue (ARR) and net new ARR added each quarter
- Net revenue retention (do existing customers spend more each year?)
- Free cash flow margin and stock-based compensation
- GAAP profitability
- Guidance for the next quarter and the full year

## What must stay true (proposed starting points: edit or delete)
1. Companies keep adding CrowdStrike modules, so ARR keeps growing at a healthy rate.
2. Customers stay (retention stays high) and trust in the product holds.

## Invalidation triggers (proposed starting points: edit or delete)
1. ARR growth slows sharply for two quarters in a row without a stated one-off cause.
2. A major product failure or breach that drives customers to competitors.
