## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold CrowdStrike because its security subscriptions keep growing fast as customers add more modules, and it turns that growth into real cash.

CrowdStrike's Falcon software guards a company's laptops, servers, cloud systems and logins. Customers start with a few modules and keep adding more, so revenue repeats and grows by itself: annual recurring revenue reached $5.84bn (+25%), and the subscriptions added in the quarter hit a record $332.8m (+51%) [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results, 2026-08-26]. Falcon Flex, a prepaid budget customers spend on modules over time, already covers over $2.29bn of that (+101%), a sign customers are committing more. AI makes security more necessary, and CrowdStrike is opening new ways to sell to AI users, such as the OpenAI Marketplace (2026-09-29). And unlike many fast-growing software companies, it brings in real cash: $377.4m of free cash flow last quarter.

**What I accept (the risks):** it is expensive (about 39-50 times yearly sales, tier-3 estimates), standard (GAAP) profit was only $5.3m, the average analyst target ($235.67) is below the recent price, and insiders sold about $210m in a few weeks (all pre-planned).

### Thesis 1: "CrowdStrike's subscriptions keep growing fast because customers keep adding modules."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** ARR grew 25% to $5.84bn; net new ARR (subscriptions added in the quarter) hit a record $332.8m, up 51%; accounts on "Falcon Flex", a prepaid budget customers spend on modules over time, are over $2.29bn of ARR, up 101% [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results, 2026-08-26].
2. **What must stay true?** ARR keeps growing at around its recent pace (25% a year), and net new ARR keeps rising from a year earlier. *(pillars 1, 2 and 3 below)*
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these *(triggers 1 and 2 below)*:
   - Net new ARR falls from a year earlier for two quarters in a row.
   - The full-year revenue forecast ($5,991-6,011m) is cut.

### Thesis 2: "AI makes security more necessary, and CrowdStrike is building new ways to sell to AI users."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Falcon is now sold through the OpenAI Marketplace (2026-09-29) [CrowdStrike, 2026-09-29], and security stocks jumped on 2026-09-14 when AI-risk warnings made investors expect more security spending [Forbes, 2026-09-14].
2. **What must stay true?** The company reports real revenue or ARR from these AI channels within the next few quarters. *(pillar 4 below)*
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these *(triggers 3 and 4 below)*:
   - No reported contribution from the AI channels after several quarters.
   - A large customer loss to a rival AI-security product.

### Thesis 3: "CrowdStrike turns its sales into real cash, even though its accounting profit is thin."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** Free cash flow (cash left after running the business and investing in it) was $377.4m last quarter, while standard (GAAP) profit was only $5.3m [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results, 2026-08-26].
2. **What must stay true?** Free cash flow stays positive and grows with revenue. *(pillar 5 below)*
3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these *(triggers 5 and 6 below)*:
   - Free cash flow falls from a year earlier for two quarters in a row.
   - GAAP losses widen while growth slows.

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
CrowdStrike sells security software called Falcon that watches a company's laptops, servers, cloud systems and logins for hackers and stops attacks. Customers pay yearly subscriptions and keep adding extra modules, so revenue repeats and grows. Its key number is ARR (annual recurring revenue: the yearly value of all subscriptions), $5.84 billion, up 25% [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results, 2026-08-26].

## How it makes money
Mostly subscriptions; the deep dive will add the split by product and region, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Annual recurring revenue (ARR) and net new ARR added each quarter
- Net revenue retention (do existing customers spend more each year?)
- Free cash flow margin and stock-based compensation
- GAAP profitability
- Guidance for the next quarter and the full year

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)
Each pillar says why it matters, how it is measured, where it stands now and when it is next checked. The daily report tests every one; when a "Now" line changes, the change log records it.

1. **Annual recurring revenue keeps growing at around 25% a year.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* ARR is the heartbeat of a subscription business: it is next year's revenue already signed.
   - *Measured by:* ARR against a year earlier, every quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $5.84bn, +25% [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results].
   - *Next check:* Q3 FY2027 results, early December (not confirmed).
2. **Subscriptions added each quarter keep rising from a year earlier.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Net new ARR shows whether growth is speeding up or slowing before the total ARR does.
   - *Measured by:* Net new ARR against the same quarter a year earlier.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Record $332.8m, +51% [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results].
   - *Next check:* Early December.
3. **Revenue lands inside the raised full-year forecast of $5,991-6,011m.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The forecast was raised in August; meeting it confirms demand is holding.
   - *Measured by:* Each quarter's revenue against guidance (Q3: $1,523.2-1,529.2m).
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Q2 revenue $1.47bn (+26%), above forecasts.
   - *Next check:* Early December.
4. **The new AI sales channels produce reported results.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* The AI reason is only real once customers pay through these channels.
   - *Measured by:* Any figure the company gives for OpenAI Marketplace customers or AI-related ARR.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Falcon listed on the OpenAI Marketplace 2026-09-29; no figures yet [CrowdStrike, 2026-09-29].
   - *Next check:* Q3 FY2027 earnings call.
5. **Free cash flow stays positive and grows with revenue.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Cash is the honest measure of profit here, because accounting profit is thin.
   - *Measured by:* Free cash flow (cash left after running and investing in the business) each quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $377.4m last quarter [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results].
   - *Next check:* Early December.
6. **Falcon Flex keeps growing.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* Flex customers prepay for future modules, which makes future growth more likely.
   - *Measured by:* ARR from accounts using Falcon Flex.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Over $2.29bn, +101% [CrowdStrike Q2 FY2027 results].
   - *Next check:* Early December.

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **Net new ARR falls from a year earlier for two quarters in a row.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* It is the earliest sign that growth is slowing for real, not just for a quarter.
   - *Counts if:* Net new ARR below the same quarter a year earlier in two consecutive quarters.
   - *Early warning:* One quarter below a year earlier.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next early December.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **The full-year revenue forecast ($5,991-6,011m) is cut.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* A cut after a raise would mean demand changed within months.
   - *Counts if:* Full-year revenue guidance below $5,991m.
   - *Early warning:* Q3 revenue below $1,523.2m (the bottom of its range).
   - *Where and when to check:* Q3 FY2027 results, early December.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **No reported contribution from the AI channels after several quarters.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Then the AI reason would be marketing, not money.
   - *Counts if:* By the Q1 FY2028 results (about June 2027) still no customer or ARR figure for the AI channels.
   - *Early warning:* Management stops mentioning them on calls.
   - *Where and when to check:* Earnings calls.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **A large customer loss to a rival AI-security product.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Losing big accounts to an AI-native rival would challenge the whole platform, not just the AI story.
   - *Counts if:* A tier-1/2 source reports a top customer moving to a rival, or the company discloses a large loss.
   - *Early warning:* Net revenue retention (spending growth of existing customers) falling, when disclosed.
   - *Where and when to check:* News in every daily report; 10-Q.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **Free cash flow falls from a year earlier for two quarters in a row.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Cash is what justifies the price while accounting profit is thin.
   - *Counts if:* Free cash flow below the same quarter a year earlier, two quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* One quarter below a year earlier.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
6. **GAAP losses widen while growth slows.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Slowing growth with rising losses would remove both the growth and the profit argument.
   - *Counts if:* A GAAP net loss in a quarter in which ARR growth also slows below 20%.
   - *Early warning:* A GAAP loss in any quarter.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
7. **ARR growth falls below 20%.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* At 39-50 times sales, the price assumes fast growth for years; below 20% would change that maths.
   - *Counts if:* ARR up less than 20% from a year earlier in any quarter.
   - *Early warning:* ARR growth of 22% or less.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
