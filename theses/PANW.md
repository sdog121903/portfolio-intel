## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Palo Alto Networks because companies are cutting the number of security suppliers they use, and Palo Alto is becoming the one they keep.

Big companies use dozens of separate security products, which is expensive and leaves gaps between them. Palo Alto's plan is to be the single platform that replaces them, and the numbers say it is working: its next-generation security subscriptions (yearly value) grew 63% to $9.10bn, and quarterly revenue grew 34% to $3.41bn, above forecasts [PANW Q4 FY2026 results, 2026-09-01]. AI adds new things to protect, such as AI agents that log in and act on their own, and Palo Alto is building products for that (Unit 42 Frontier AI Defense, launched 2026-09-22). Buying CyberArk for $21.1bn added control of logins for people and AI agents, the part of security that is hardest for a customer to rip out. It is also my biggest-but-one position, so the case needs to be strong.

**What I accept (the risks):** the price is high (about 96-97 times next year's expected adjusted profit per share), growth is guided to slow to 23-24% as the CyberArk boost fades, it lost money under standard accounting last quarter, and the deal added 112 million new shares.

### Thesis 1: "Companies want fewer security suppliers, and Palo Alto is becoming the one-stop platform they consolidate on."

3. **What would prove me wrong?** Any one of these *(triggers 1 and 2 below)*:
   - The yearly revenue forecast ($14.10-14.20bn) is cut.

### Thesis 2: "AI creates new things to protect (AI agents and models), and Palo Alto is building products for that."

1. **Why would I own it for this?** It launched AI-security services such as Unit 42 Frontier AI Defense (2026-09-22), and security stocks jumped about 13% on 2026-09-14 when AI-risk warnings made investors expect more security spending [Forbes, 2026-09-14].
2. **What must stay true?** AI-security products start to show up in the ARR and revenue the company reports. *(pillar 3 below)*
3. **What would prove me wrong?** After several quarters the company still reports no traction for its AI-security products, or customers choose rivals for AI security. *(trigger 3 below)*

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Palo Alto Networks sells cybersecurity: protection for company networks, cloud systems and logins, more and more as subscriptions that customers renew every year. It wants to be a "platform", meaning customers buy many security products from one company instead of from many small ones. Its key number, next-generation security ARR (annual recurring revenue: the yearly value of its newer subscriptions), reached $9.10 billion, up 63%, partly because it bought CyberArk [PANW Q4 FY2026 results, 2026-09-01].

## How it makes money
Product sales (firewall hardware) plus subscriptions and support, which are the growing part.
The deep dive will add the split, with sources.

## Metrics that matter
- Next-generation security ARR (annual recurring revenue from its newer products)
- Remaining performance obligation (contracted revenue not yet recognised)
- Free cash flow margin
- Guidance for the year

## What must stay true (checkable pillars)
Each pillar says why it matters, how it is measured, where it stands now and when it is next checked. The daily report tests every one; when a "Now" line changes, the change log records it.

1. **Next-generation security subscriptions keep growing after the CyberArk boost fades.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* These subscriptions are the platform working: customers adding products, not one-off hardware sales.
   - *Measured by:* Next-generation security ARR (yearly value of subscriptions) against a year earlier, every quarter.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* $9.10bn, up 63% including CyberArk [PANW Q4 FY2026 results].
   - *Next check:* Q1 FY2027 results, mid-to-late November (not confirmed).
2. **Yearly revenue lands inside the $14.10-14.20bn forecast.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The forecast already includes the slowdown; missing even that would mean the platform story is weakening.
   - *Measured by:* Each quarter's revenue against guidance (Q1: $3.300-3.310bn) and the full-year range.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Q4 revenue $3.41bn (+34%), above forecasts [PANW Q4 FY2026 results].
   - *Next check:* Q1 FY2027 results, mid-to-late November.
3. **AI-security products start to show up in reported numbers.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* Without numbers, "security for AI" is only a story; with them it is a growth engine.
   - *Measured by:* Any customer count, ARR or revenue the company discloses for its AI-security products.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Launched 2026-09-22; no figures yet.
   - *Next check:* Q1 FY2027 earnings call.
4. **CyberArk keeps growing inside Palo Alto.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Identity security (who and what may log in) is the hardest part for a customer to replace, so it locks in the platform.
   - *Measured by:* Identity-security growth when disclosed; no write-down of the purchase; no integration problems named on calls.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* Purchase completed 2026-02-11 for $21.1bn [Palo Alto Networks, 2026-02-11].
   - *Next check:* Each 10-Q and earnings call.
5. **The share count stops growing quickly.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* Each new share makes my slice of the company smaller; the CyberArk deal already added 112 million.
   - *Measured by:* Diluted share count in each 10-Q against the previous one.
   - *Now (as of 2026-10-04):* 112 million new shares issued for CyberArk [PANW 10-Q, 2026-06-02].
   - *Next check:* Next 10-Q (with Q1 FY2027 results).

## Invalidation triggers (what would prove the thesis wrong)
Each trigger says why it matters, exactly what counts, the early warning that usually comes first, where to look and its status now. A trigger that fires becomes a THESIS ALERT in the daily report: a reason to re-read this file, not an instruction to sell. An early warning is reported as "Watch".

1. **The yearly revenue forecast ($14.10-14.20bn) is cut.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* The company's own forecast is the clearest test of whether customers keep consolidating on it.
   - *Counts if:* Full-year FY2027 revenue guidance below $14.10bn.
   - *Early warning:* A quarter at or below the bottom of its range (Q1: $3.300bn), or the range narrowed to its low end.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results; next mid-to-late November.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
2. **ARR growth falls well below the company's own plan for two quarters in a row.** *(Thesis 1)*
   - *Why it matters:* Subscriptions are the platform; two weak quarters in a row would not be bad luck.
   - *Counts if:* Management lowers its subscription (ARR) outlook, or next-generation security ARR shrinks from the previous quarter, two quarters in a row.
   - *Early warning:* Subscriptions added in a quarter fall below the same quarter a year earlier.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results and calls.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
3. **After several quarters the company still reports no traction for its AI-security products, or customers choose rivals for AI security.** *(Thesis 2)*
   - *Why it matters:* The AI-security reason only holds if customers actually buy.
   - *Counts if:* By the Q3 FY2027 results (about May 2027) no customer, ARR or revenue figure for AI security has been given; or a tier-1/2 source reports big customers picking a rival.
   - *Early warning:* Management stops mentioning the AI products on calls.
   - *Where and when to check:* Earnings calls; news in every daily report.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
4. **A write-down of the CyberArk purchase (admitting it was worth less than paid).** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* A write-down means the most expensive deal in its history is not delivering.
   - *Counts if:* An impairment charge (an accounting write-down) tied to CyberArk in a 10-Q or 10-K.
   - *Early warning:* Identity-security growth slows sharply, or senior CyberArk managers leave.
   - *Where and when to check:* Each 10-Q and 10-K.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
5. **Guidance cut blamed on integration problems.** *(Thesis 3)*
   - *Why it matters:* Combining two big companies can distract the sales force, which hurts growth for several quarters.
   - *Counts if:* Any guidance cut where management names CyberArk or the integration as a cause.
   - *Early warning:* Management mentions sales disruption or slower deals linked to the merger.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results and calls.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit.
6. **Standard (GAAP) losses widen for two quarters in a row.** *(whole position; added 2026-10-05)*
   - *Why it matters:* At about 96-97 times expected adjusted profit, the price needs real profits to arrive, not deepen.
   - *Counts if:* GAAP net loss per share bigger than the previous quarter's, two quarters in a row (last quarter: -$0.35).
   - *Early warning:* One quarter with a bigger GAAP loss.
   - *Where and when to check:* Quarterly results.
   - *Status now (2026-10-04):* Not hit (one GAAP loss quarter so far, -$0.35).
