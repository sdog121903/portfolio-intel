## Why I own it (approved by Santi, 2026-10-05)

**In one sentence:** I hold Bloom Energy because AI data centers cannot wait years for the power grid, and Bloom's fuel cells let them make their own power on site.

Power is now one of the biggest bottlenecks for AI: new data centers can wait years for a grid connection. Bloom's fuel cells turn natural gas into electricity on site without burning it, so a data center can start running sooner; that is a different way to profit from AI than chips or software. The demand is showing up: revenue grew 165.5% to $1,065.4m last quarter and the 2026 outlook was raised to $3.9-4.2bn [Bloom Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-28], and utilities are buying too (Ameren plans 500 megawatts, per Barclays, tier 3). The business has also moved from burning cash to bringing it in ($226.4m of operating cash flow). Its biggest project, Oracle's Project Jupiter (up to 2.45 gigawatts), is delayed by a gas pipeline but, all sides say, still under contract [CNBC, 2026-09-24].

**What I accept (the risks):** it is very jumpy (worst fall 52.7% in a year), a shareholder lawsuit is pending over a short-seller report Bloom calls false, and the price is about 108 times expected profit (tier 3).

## What the company does (plain English, 3 sentences)
Bloom Energy makes fuel cells: refrigerator-sized boxes that turn natural gas into electricity on site through a chemical reaction instead of burning it. Data centers buy them because the power grid cannot connect new sites fast enough, so they make their own power. Revenue was $1,065.4 million last quarter, up 165.5% [Bloom Q2 2026 results, 2026-07-28].

## How it makes money
Selling and installing power systems, plus long-term service contracts to run and maintain them.
